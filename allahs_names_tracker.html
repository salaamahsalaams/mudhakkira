Ep 01

How Merciful is the Most Merciful?

Watch

Names Covered

Ar-Raḥmān · Ar-Raḥīm · Ar-Ra'ūf

Theme

Mercy as the governing principle of all creation

The Names

الرَّحْمَنُ

Ar-Raḥmān

The Entirely Merciful

الرَّحِيمُ

Ar-Raḥīm

The Especially Merciful

الرَّءُوفُ

Ar-Ra'ūf

The Tenderly Compassionate

Background

Mercy is the first thing Allah wants you to know about Him — 113 surahs open with it. Before creation, He wrote above His Throne: "My mercy prevails over My wrath."

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الرَّحْمَنُ Ar-Raḥmān Universal mercy that covers all creation without distinction — believer and disbeliever alike. Governs everything beneath the Throne.
الرَّحِيمُ Ar-Raḥīm Personal, reserved mercy specifically for believers. 99 out of 100 portions of mercy are saved for them in the Hereafter.
الرَّءُوفُ Ar-Ra'ūf Preemptive mercy — protects you before harm even reaches you. Works behind the scenes to shield you from what you never knew was coming.

Key Points

PointDetail
Mercy is His governing attribute He chose to introduce Himself with mercy before any other quality — 113 surahs open with it. Forgiveness is more beloved to Him than punishment, giving more beloved than withholding. Every other name operates under this one.
Cruelty is a distribution failure, not a supply problem He divided mercy into 100 portions and sent just one to earth — that single portion sustains every act of kindness in all of creation. We are the trustees of that share. The remaining 99 are saved for believers in the Hereafter.
Mercy multiplies when you practice it Show mercy to those around you and it draws more mercy toward you from above. Mercy is not just something you receive — it is something you actively reflect, and reflecting it is how you access more of it.

Takeaway & Du'ā

Mercy is not Allah's mood — it is His constant nature. Wherever you are, whatever you carry, you are something, and His mercy encompasses all things.

يَا رَحْمَنُ

Your mercy is wider than everything I have ever done wrong, and greater than any mistake I have yet to make. Cover every part of me in it — my past, my present, and whatever is still to come.

يَا رَحِيمُ

Let me taste the mercy You keep for those who return. Reward me with the mercy I have yet to know, and raise me to the highest place in Paradise despite my lows.

يَا رَءُوفُ

Cover me from the storms I do not see coming. Mend me before I break. Spare me from trials of every kind, and let Your mercy reach me in ways I will only fully understand when I finally meet You.

Ep 02

Who Owns Your Heart?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Wāḥid · Al-Aḥad · Al-Witr

Theme

Tawḥīd as liberation — unifying the heart under one God

The Names

الْوَاحِدُ

Al-Wāḥid

The One — singular in being

الْأَحَدُ

Al-Aḥad

The Uniquely One — singular in attributes

الْوِتْرُ

Al-Witr

The Odd — the only one of His kind

Background

Whatever your heart completely revolves around becomes your god. Allah placed only one heart in your chest because it was made for one master.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الْوَاحِدُ Al-Wāḥid Singular in being — negates numbers. There is no second God, period.
الْأَحَدُ Al-Aḥad Singular in attributes — negates likeness. Not just "one" but "incomparably one." Nothing resembles Him in any way.
الْوِتْرُ Al-Witr Unpaired. Everything in creation comes in pairs — day/night, male/female. He alone stands without a pair or counterpart.

Key Points

PointDetail
Whatever controls your heart is your god A god is whatever your heart completely revolves around — career, validation, money, approval. Identify what has god-like control over your decisions and you will find what you truly worship.
Divided devotion means losing everything Allah leaves you to whatever you associate with Him. A unified heart under one Master is the only path to freedom. A divided heart serves everything and is owned by nothing.
Tawhid is practiced daily, not just believed Odd-number rituals — Witr prayer, odd dates at iftar, seven circuits of Tawaf — are constant reminders that He alone stands without pair. Oneness is lived through action, not just affirmed in belief.

Takeaway & Du'ā

When others scatter their devotion across a thousand things, direct yours to the One — and discover what it means to finally be free from all.

يَا وَاحِدُ

Unify my pursuits into a single direction. When the world pulls me apart, centre me in Your oneness.

يَا أَحَدُ

Open my heart to see that nothing compares to You, so that I am never overly impressed by anyone but You, and I come to love Your names in a way that frees me from all else.

يَا وِتْرُ

When I stand alone at the close of the night, let my solitude remind me of Your singular majesty. Make my heart distinct by its devotion, and make me one of Your rarest servants.

Ep 03

Who Shows You the Way?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Hādī · Ar-Rashīd · An-Nūr · Al-Mubīn

Theme

Guidance as a gift — from confusion to clarity to light

The Names

الْهَادِي

Al-Hādī

The Guide

الرَّشِيدُ

Ar-Rashīd

The Director of Right Guidance

النُّورُ

An-Nūr

The Light

الْمُبِينُ

Al-Mubīn

The Self-Evident

Background

Guidance is not automatic — you have to pursue it. He named Himself the Guide, not the misguider. The door is already open; you just have to walk through it.

The Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الْهَادِي Al-Hādī Built guidance into all of creation. Seeking Him sincerely and not being guided is a contradiction.
الرَّشِيدُ Ar-Rashīd Guidance so deep it becomes instinct — He rewires not just your knowledge but your desires. Faith feels natural, sin feels foreign.
النُّورُ An-Nūr The light by which the heart recognises truth. Without it, you look at signs and see nothing; with it, everything carries meaning.
الْمُبِينُ Al-Mubīn Makes truth plain — His signs are everywhere for those whose hearts carry His light. What He reveals is not hidden; it is the sight that may be limited.

Key Points

PointDetail
Guidance is His gift — but you must pursue it Guidance comes when you turn toward Him; misguidance is His justice when you insist on turning away. The door is open — you have to walk through it.
Guidance deepens from knowledge to instinct He rewires your desires so faith feels natural and sin feels heavy — not just intellectually wrong but spiritually uncomfortable. You stop dragging yourself to worship and start being drawn to it.
Never write anyone off Guidance can flip suddenly in anyone. We see surfaces; Allah sees what is ready inside a person's heart. That includes you — no matter where you are right now.

Takeaway & Du'ā

There are countless ways to get lost. There is only one way home. He already lit the path — keep walking towards Him.

يَا هَادِي

Guide me when my heart forgets the way. Pull me back from every turn that leads me astray. Let Your guidance feel closer than the doubt in my chest and steadier than the voices that confuse me.

يَا رَشِيدُ

Teach me to see truth instinctively. Make faith beloved to me and righteousness feel natural. Let sin feel heavy on my chest and unwelcome to my soul. Inspire me with wisdom that protects me even from myself.

يَا نُورُ

Light every darkness I carry within. Let Your light pour into my heart and onto all my limbs, until You make me a light and everything I see reminds me of You.

يَا مُبِينُ

Make Your signs clear in what surrounds me and in what is within me. Reveal the lessons hidden in every trial. Let me never lose sight of Your signs again.

Ep 04

Who Never Leaves You?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Barr · Al-Wālī · Ar-Rafīq

Theme

Allah as the eternal companion — stable, protective, and gentle

The Names

الْبَرُّ

Al-Barr

The Constant Source of All Good

الْوَلِيُّ

Al-Wālī

The Protective Friend

الرَّفِيقُ

Ar-Rafīq

The Gentle Companion

Background

Every human companion is temporary. Only Allah combines all the qualities of a perfect friend in one: stability, protection, and gentleness.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الْبَرُّ Al-Barr The constant, stable source of all good. Every human friend carries storms; He is the only ground that does not shift beneath you.
الْوَلِيُّ Al-Wālī The protective friend who guides and guards simultaneously — no gap between you and His care, through every phase of life.
الرَّفِيقُ Ar-Rafīq The gentle companion who applies exactly the right touch at the right moment. Sometimes lifts the difficulty; sometimes makes the weight easier to carry.

Key Points

PointDetail
Only Allah combines every quality of a perfect companion Stability, protection, and gentleness — no human can offer all three consistently. People tire of you leaning on them; He loves it when you cling to Him.
Closeness deepens through private devotion Through consistent worship, Allah becomes the lens through which you hear, see, and act. This deeper rank of closeness comes through private devotion, not public performance.
His companionship continues past the grave When every human companion has gone, He remains. He sends your good deeds ahead to keep you company, and on the Day when former friends turn on each other, His close ones are escorted to the shade of His Throne.

Takeaway & Du'ā

Every companion will eventually depart. Until then — and especially then — direct your heart above and say: Allāhumma ar-Rafīq al-Aʿlā.

يَا بَرُّ

Keep me firm on the ground of Your goodness. Make my faith steady when my heart trembles. Let me love what brings me to Your stability and make me patient with what keeps me on its shore.

يَا وَلِيُّ

Be my closest friend when the world drifts away. Guard me with the grip that never slips. Guide me gently through what I do not understand, and let every loss lead me back to You.

يَا رَفِيقُ

Be tender with my soul as You unfold Your plan. Soften the path without removing its purpose. Make me gentle with others as You have been gentle with me. And when I am lonely, fill that space with Your company.

Ep 05

Who Do You Belong To?

Watch

Names Covered

Ar-Rabb · Al-Mawlā · An-Naṣīr · As-Sayyid

Theme

Servitude as liberation — the Lord who raises, guards, and grants victory

The Names

الرَّبُّ

Ar-Rabb

The Lord Who Raises

الْمَوْلَى

Al-Mawlā

The Protecting Master

النَّصِيرُ

An-Naṣīr

The Helper

السَّيِّدُ

As-Sayyid

The Master of All Masters

Background

Belonging to Allah is not humiliation — it is the only path to true freedom from everything else. Friendship brings comfort, but servitude brings purpose.

The Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الرَّبُّ Ar-Rabb Nurtures you toward your full potential. Every hardship is measured and every ease is timed precisely for your growth.
الْمَوْلَى Al-Mawlā Exclusively on the side of believers — present throughout the journey, especially in moments of desperation.
النَّصِيرُ An-Naṣīr Guarantees the outcome. While Mawla is with you throughout the journey, Nasir delivers the victory — sometimes by changing the world around you, sometimes by changing you from within.
السَّيِّدُ As-Sayyid The Master above all masters. Every leader's power is borrowed; His alone is original. Remove His authority and every command dissolves.

Key Points

PointDetail
Servitude to Allah is freedom from everything else The highest title a person can carry is servant of Allah — because it liberates you from serving anyone else. Every other form of servitude diminishes you; this one elevates you.
His cultivation of you is intentional Every hardship, ease, and season in your life is precisely measured for your growth. He does not just keep you alive — He is actively developing you toward your full potential.
The King invites you every night He calls out nightly offering to respond, give, and forgive — no guards, no ceremony, no conditions. The same authority that will declare sovereignty on the Last Day opens His door to you every single night.

Takeaway & Du'ā

Belonging to Him is not a burden — it is the only path to true freedom from all else.

يَا رَبُّ

Raise me the way You raise every seed from the soil. Shape my growth with all that You continue to give. Let every season You place me in become a lesson in Your care.

يَا مَوْلَى

Be my protector when I have no one else to stand for me. Take charge of my affairs when I lose control of them. Hold me near when fear surrounds me, and make Your guardianship always enough.

يَا نَصِيرُ

Help me in every battle I cannot see. Grant me triumph without arrogance, and victory only in a way that brings me closer to You.

يَا سَيِّدُ

Teach my heart that true honour is only in serving You. Let my dignity be in humility, my leadership in obedience, and my freedom in surrender.

Ep 06

Who is the King?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Malik · Al-Maalik · Al-Maleek · Al-Jaleel

Theme

All authority on earth is borrowed — His alone is original and absolute

The Names

الْمَلِكُ

Al-Malik

The King

الْمَالِكُ

Al-Maalik

The Owner

الْمَلِيكُ

Al-Maleek

The Sovereign

الْجَلِيلُ

Al-Jaleel

The Majestic

Background

The Quran bookends your life with Rabb and Malik — Rabb addresses the personal side of His care (the one who nurtures), while Malik addresses the public side of His authority (the one who governs all).

The Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الْمَلِكُ Al-Malik The King whose authority is not acquired, inherited, or voted in — it simply is and always will be. No one placed a crown on Him and no one can remove it.
الْمَالِكُ Al-Maalik Owns everything outright — death, life, your loved ones, even you. He does not just reign; He owns. Everything you hold is on loan from Him.
الْمَلِيكُ Al-Maleek Sovereignty so complete His decree reaches every corner — not just the power to rule but the power to execute with no possibility of resistance.
الْجَلِيلُ Al-Jaleel Majesty that causes reverence from within. His greatness humbles without humiliating and draws you closer rather than pushing you away.

Key Points

PointDetail
All human authority is borrowed and temporary Any authority that forgets who placed it there is already on the path to corruption. Every empire that carved its name in stone has returned to dirt — the lesson remains; the kings are gone.
The Day of Judgement is when all titles expire On that Day He declares: "I am the King." Every worldly king stands as a servant, every claim of ownership dissolves. He placed this name right after His namess of mercy in Surah al-Fatiha — so you never mistake Him for a harsh king.
The King invites you every night Every night He calls out: "Who is asking so I may respond? Who wants something so I can give? Who seeks forgiveness so I can forgive?" Answer now with humility, and that final call becomes mercy for you.

Takeaway & Du'ā

The same King who will roll up the heavens and earth on the last Day opens His door to you every single night. Answer Him now.

يَا مَلِكُ

You are the King of everything I know and hold. Remind me that nothing I hold is truly mine and that every rise and fall belongs to Your decree. Let my gratitude grow with every breath You lend to me.

يَا مَالِكُ

Owner of the Day when every debt is finally paid. Teach me to settle what I owe before I meet You. Keep me honest with what I carry, and hopeful in what You forgive.

يَا مَلِيكُ

Your command reaches everywhere and no one escapes it. Let that truth settle into my pride and dissolve it. Make me content under Your rule and protect me from a heart that would resist You.

يَا جَلِيلُ

Fill my heart with a reverence that draws me near, not a fear that drives me away. Let my awe of You refine me — until I stand before You small, but never disgraced.

Ep 07

Who Made You?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Khāliq · Al-Bāri' · Al-Muṣawwir · Al-Khallāq

Theme

Creation with purpose — from existence to essence to beauty to renewal

The Names

الْخَالِقُ

Al-Khāliq

The Creator

الْبَارِئُ

Al-Bāri'

The Producer

الْمُصَوِّرُ

Al-Muṣawwir

The Fashioner

الْخَلَّاقُ

Al-Khallāq

The Constant Creator

Background

You are not here by accident. Out of every possibility that could have existed, He chose you — at this time, in this place, to these parents, for a purpose that only you can fulfill.

The Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الْخَالِقُ Al-Khāliq Creates out of nothing, but nothing without purpose. Even a single seed carries an entire curriculum — when to sprout, how deep to root, how to harvest light. Only He can write DNA into dust.
الْبَارِئُ Al-Bāri' Produces the inner reality — your soul and essence — then repairs it when it breaks. Repentance to Him is not just apology; it is asking the manufacturer to restore you to the noble image He intended.
الْمُصَوِّرُ Al-Muṣawwir The artist of every detail — your eyes, your smile, your smallest birthmark. He clothed your bones with flesh before anyone could clothe your body. True beauty is in sincerity, not symmetry.
الْخَلَّاقُ Al-Khallāq Did not create you once and close your file. New cells form, blood replenishes, skin regenerates — and in your story, He creates new openings where you once saw only walls.

Key Points

PointDetail
Your existence is deliberate, not accidental Your ancestors survived wars, plagues, and famines. Out of billions of people your parents met, and out of a million possibilities, you were chosen. A million things could have gone wrong — but they didn't. That is not random; that is Al-Khāliq.
The one who made you knows how to mend you He holds the manufacturer's warranty on your soul. He can restore the purity you think you've lost and the hope you buried. He even repurposes flaws into strengths — He turned ʿUmar's reckless power into a force for justice that changed the world.
He did not create you once and leave That text you needed, the person who suddenly appeared, the path that wasn't there and then it was — none of it is coincidence. That is creation in motion. He keeps writing new beginnings until He writes the final chapter.

Takeaway & Du'ā

You are a masterpiece signed by the greatest artist of all. He does not make mistakes — and He never stops creating.

يَا خَالِقُ

You brought me out of nothingness into being. Remind me that my existence itself is a mercy. That every breath, every cell, and every chance is a deliberate act of Your creation.

يَا بَارِئُ

The One who perfects what He produces. Repair what I have broken within myself. Make me whole again with the same precision by which You fashioned all of Your creation.

يَا مُصَوِّرُ

You shaped me in the unseen. Gave me form, face, and story. Beautify my character as You have beautified my features. Let what You see within me be more pleasing than what others see outside of me.

يَا خَلَّاقُ

Keep creating new openings when I run out of ways. Create for me new beginnings when I rush to my end, and new realities when I feel doomed by my sins. Remake me with patience when I reach my limits, faith when I am afraid, and hope when I cannot imagine a way forward.

Ep 08

What Makes You Whole?

Watch

Names Covered

Aṣ-Ṣamad · Al-Ḥaqq · Al-Wāsiʿ

Theme

From emptiness to truth to vastness — only He can fill what nothing else can

The Names

الصَّمَدُ

Aṣ-Ṣamad

The Self-Sufficient

الْحَقُّ

Al-Ḥaqq

The Truth

الْوَاسِعُ

Al-Wāsiʿ

The Vast

Background

Allah lets you feel empty and dissatisfied with everything else so that you don't depend too much on those things. Every void you feel is a signal from your soul pointing you back to the only One who can truly fill it.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الصَّمَدُ Aṣ-Ṣamad The one who is whole, not hollow — completely self-sufficient and free of all need. He has no gaps, no parts that can break apart. He is the perfect refuge and the only one who can make you feel whole, because He alone is entirely free of need.
الْحَقُّ Al-Ḥaqq Not just truthful, but truth itself — in His being, His promise, His word, and His meeting. Everything connected to Him has lasting value; everything detached is hollow and temporary. Each truth He reveals unlocks the next, building a roadmap of purpose.
الْوَاسِعُ Al-Wāsiʿ Vast in every way with no scarcity. His capacity is infinite — no matter how many call on Him, He hears every voice. He never runs out of mercy, patience, or provision. He is the divine expander who opens your knowledge, capacity, and blessings when you walk toward Him.

Key Points

PointDetail
You were designed to feel empty without Him Sins make you feel miserable and worldly pursuits leave you unfulfilled — by design. A job gives income but not meaning. A relationship gives companionship but not completion. Fame gives attention but not validation. Every created thing has a limit, and the moment you reach it, the void returns.
Truth is a roadmap — each truth unlocks the next Once you know Allah is real, you trust His promise. Then His word is true, then meeting Him is true, then paradise and hellfire are true, then the prophets are true. The reward of a good deed is another good deed and more clarity. But the punishment of sin is spiritual claustrophobia — the path narrows and you feel trapped.
Restriction for His sake opens you to His vastness Praying Fajr is hard. Lowering your gaze is tough. Fasting on a hot day is exhausting. But that very restraint opens a world you could not otherwise access. People suffocate in mansions, choked by wealth, trapped by their liberating sins. Every sacrifice for Allah has a higher yield — every restriction is temporary and replaced by a promised permanent reward.

Takeaway & Du'ā

Return to Aṣ-Ṣamad for wholeness when you fall apart, to Al-Ḥaqq for meaning when you lose your way, and to Al-Wāsiʿ for space when the world feels small.

يَا صَمَدُ

The One who needs nothing while everything needs You. Make me whole by depending only on You. Fill the hollowness inside of me until no desire within me competes with Your glory.

يَا حَقُّ

Make Your truth the anchor of my heart and my guiding path to Your light. Let every lie I chase collapse before Your reality. And every truth I seek, lead me to Your next promise. Keep me grounded in what endures and turn my eyes away from what deceives.

يَا وَاسِعُ

Expand my heart to hold more gratitude, my patience to endure with more grace, and my vision to see beyond my limits. Let me find expanse in Your vastness when the world around me starts to feel small.

Ep 09

Who Forgives You?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Ghāfir · Al-Ghaffār · Al-Ghafūr · At-Tawwāb

Theme

The layers of Allah's forgiveness — from covering a single sin to welcoming persistent return

The Names

الغَافِرُ

Al-Ghāfir

The Forgiver

الغَفَّارُ

Al-Ghaffār

The Oft-Forgiving

الغَفُورُ

Al-Ghafūr

The All-Forgiving

التَّوَّابُ

At-Tawwāb

The Acceptor of Repentance

Background

On the Day of Judgment, Allah will bring a servant near, place a veil between him and everyone else, and remind him of his sins one by one. When the servant thinks there is no hope left, Allah says: I concealed it for you in this life, and today I have forgiven you. The root of مَغْفِرَة means to cover and protect — forgiveness is not just erasure, but armor that shields you in both worlds.

Distinguishing the Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الغَافِرُ Al-Ghāfir The simplest form — a single sin met with guaranteed forgiveness when you repent. "The forgiver of sin, the acceptor of repentance." He opens the door the moment you knock.
الغَفَّارُ Al-Ghaffār The oft-forgiving — emphasises the quantity of forgiveness. When you stumble again and again, He still does not shut His door on you. Every time you return, He meets you with forgiveness once more.
الغَفُورُ Al-Ghafūr The all-encompassing forgiver — emphasises the quality. His ability to forgive sins no matter how enormous, including sins you never even realised you committed. The most frequently mentioned of these names in the Qur'an, matching the frequency of our sins.
التَّوَّابُ At-Tawwāb The one who turns hearts back to Him so He can then turn towards them. He inspires repentance before you even ask, sends reminders, and opens pathways back. Then He rewards you for doing what you should have done anyway.

Key Points

PointDetail
Forgiveness is armor, not just erasure مِغْفَر (mighfar) — a helmet — shares the same root as مَغْفِرَة. When you ask for forgiveness you are asking Allah to cover the sin and protect you from its consequences. He covers your sins in this world so others cannot humiliate you, and covers them in the next so you are not exposed when everyone is desperate for mercy.
Sin can become an invitation, not a disqualification A sin that brings you closer to Allah can be better than a good deed that breeds arrogance. A door of sin that opens a door of humility, brokenness, and sincere return can outweigh many acts of obedience. The story of humanity itself began with a sin — and that sin of Adam (AS) did not destroy the story, it completed it.
Allah moves the earth to forgive — and turns your dark ink into light A man killed 100 people, repented, and died on the journey to a new land. Allah commanded the earth itself to bring him closer to the land of mercy — He inspired the repentance, sent the scholar, and shifted the ground, all so He could forgive him. And He replaces evil deeds with good deeds: the same servant whose face was changing colours from doom gets so excited seeing his sins transformed that he says: "Ya Allah, the angel forgot to write down some of my sins."

Takeaway & Du'ā

Don't despair of His mercy — not because you deserve it, but because He defines Himself by it. Return to Al-Ghāfir for the first slip, to Al-Ghaffār for repeated falls, to Al-Ghafūr for sins seen and unseen, and to At-Tawwāb for the pull that draws you back.

يَا غَافِرُ

I sinned, forgive me.

يَا غَفَّارُ

I did it again, even though I said I wouldn't, forgive me.

يَا غَفُورُ

For the sins I know and don't know, forgive me. Cover me in this life and the next. Don't let shame keep me from returning to You. Wash away every hidden trace of my wrongdoing and turn the stains of my past into light on my scrolls.

يَا تَوَّابُ

Turn towards me so that I may turn back to You. Call me back every time I drift. Accept me every time I return. Make repentance my constant companion and joy and Jannah my reward in finding You again.

Ep 10

Who Loves You?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Wadūd · Al-Muḥsin · Ash-Shakūr

Theme

Divine love, excellence in good, and gratitude — how Allah loves and acts upon His love, outdoes you in good, and rewards even the smallest deed beyond measure

The Names

الوَدُودُ

Al-Wadūd

The Loving

المُحْسِنُ

Al-Muḥsin

The Ultimate Good-Doer

الشَّكُورُ

Ash-Shakūr

The Most Appreciative

Background

Does Allah love everyone? The answer is more nuanced than a simple yes or no. He doesn't love the persistent sinner and the striving good-doer in the same way — but He loves good for you and He loves repentance from you. Shu'ayb (AS) told his people: "Seek your Lord's forgiveness, then turn back to Him. Indeed, my Lord is merciful and loving." And Allah says: "If you truly love Allah, then follow me and Allah will love you back and forgive your sins." So Allah's love is expensive and unique — but not inaccessible. Follow the Ḥabīb ﷺ and you enter the circle of Al-Wadūd.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الوَدُودُ Al-Wadūd The one who loves and acts upon His love. وُدّ is love translated into action — unlike حُبّ which can be a mere feeling. When He loves you, He announces it through the heavens: He calls Jibrīl and says "I love so-and-so, love them too," then Jibrīl announces it to the angels, and that love descends to earth into the lives of people you may not have even met.
المُحْسِنُ Al-Muḥsin The ultimate good-doer. إِحْسَان means doing more than what's expected — and Allah will never let you outdo Him in good. For your half-distracted prayers, your fasting, your charity that didn't sting too much, He gives you Jannah. He takes your one dollar and turns it into mountains of gold. The tasbīḥ that barely cost you a breath earns you a palace in Jannah.
الشَّكُورُ Ash-Shakūr The most appreciative. Just as Al-Ghafūr forgives beyond measure, Ash-Shakūr rewards beyond measure. He appreciates even your smallest deed, while most humans deny the most significant good done to them. The Prophet ﷺ stood in prayer until his feet swelled — though already forgiven — and said: "Shall I not be an extremely grateful servant (ʿabdan shakūrā)?"

Key Points

PointDetail
Love translated into action When Allah loves you, it never stops at feelings. He calls Jibrīl (AS) and says: "I love so-and-so, so love them." Then Jibrīl announces it to the angels, and that love descends to earth — like a current running from the Throne of Ar-Raḥmān through the angels of light into the lives of people around you. وُدّ is to love someone and constantly look for them and do what they love.
Love can become a false idol Loving something or someone equally to Allah leads to conforming to what it wants of you — that is شِرْك المَحَبَّة, associating partners in love. Al-Wadūd is willing to love, but you must be loyal in that love. The Prophet ﷺ taught us to ask: "O Allah, I ask You for Your love, the love of those who love You, and the love of every deed that brings me nearer to Your love."
Al-Muḥsin and Ash-Shakūr will never be outmatched A man had no good deeds except overlooking debts — Allah said: "We are more worthy of this leniency than him," and forgave everything. Whoever relieves a Muslim of a burden, Allah relieves him on the Day of Judgment. Even a lifelong sinner who gave water to a dog was forgiven entirely. And Allah gives worldly blessings to those He loves and those He doesn't — but He only gives faith (īmān) to those He loves. Gratitude transforms worship from bare-minimum obligation to extra loving devotion: "If you are grateful, I will increase you even more."

Takeaway & Du'ā

Follow the Ḥabīb ﷺ and enter the circle of Al-Wadūd. Let your gratitude transform bare-minimum worship into loving devotion. Know that your smallest act of good is met by Al-Muḥsin with more than you could ever imagine, and your faintest whisper of thanks is received by Ash-Shakūr with a reward beyond measure.

يَا وَدُودُ

Love me even after I falter. Teach me to love you more than what you give, and announce your love for me in the heavens until I hear it myself in paradise.

يَا مُحْسِنُ

You perfect everything you touch. Make me one of those who do good because you do good. Guide me to the ways of good in every single one of my dealings. Then let my deeds of excellence to others be a reflection of your unmatched perfection.

يَا شَكُورُ

For every small effort I make, reward me beyond measure. Let me never grow tired of thanking you, and never forget that gratitude itself is a gift from you. Accept from me what is broken, and multiply it until it becomes whole.

Who Hears You?

Watch

Names Covered

As-Samīʿ · Al-Qarīb · Al-Mujīb

Theme

Divine hearing, closeness, and responsiveness — how Allah hears before you speak, is closer than you think, and answers before you finish asking

The Names

السَّمِيعُ

As-Samīʿ

The All-Hearing

القَرِيبُ

Al-Qarīb

The Ever-Near

المُجِيبُ

Al-Mujīb

The Responsive

Background

Before your lips moved, He was already listening. "And when My servants ask you about Me, I am close" — in every other question the Qur'an says "Say" (قُلْ), but here there is no قُلْ — just فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ, as Allah Himself intervenes to answer directly.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
السَّمِيعُ As-Samīʿ Hearing is not effort for Him but an attribute of His essence. He hears every voice and even what hasn't become sound yet — يَعْلَمُ السِّرَّ وَأَخْفَى. Your silent du'ā in the dark is itself proof of faith.
القَرِيبُ Al-Qarīb Close to everyone in awareness, close to the believer in mercy. "We are closer to him than his jugular vein." Qurbānī (sacrifice) comes from qurb (closeness) — each act of sacrifice is a step nearer to Him.
المُجِيبُ Al-Mujīb He answers what you ask and what you mean. Sometimes by protecting you from evil, sometimes with exactly what you asked for, sometimes by changing you before your circumstances — because that was better all along.

Key Points

PointDetail
Zakariyyā's silent cry He called upon Allah with a نِدَاءً خَفِيًّا — a "quiet shout." Before he could even finish, Allah cut him off with the answer: a son named Yaḥyā, already named and shaped. His forefather Ibrāhīm had used the same words: إِنَّ رَبِّي لَسَمِيعُ الدُّعَاءِ.
Closeness demands sacrifice Ibrāhīm laid down his son. Āsiyah looked past Fir'awn's throne and asked for a house with Allah, not just in Jannah. The magicians who once longed for Fir'awn's closeness fell in sujūd to Allah instead. Qurb isn't a concept you talk about — it's a closeness you live.
Qurb before ijābah إِنَّ رَبِّي قَرِيبٌ مُّجِيبٌ — Al-Qarīb comes before Al-Mujīb. He brings you close first so He can answer you in the best way. Imām Aḥmad said the distance between us and the Throne is "a sincere prayer from a pure heart."

Takeaway & Du'ā

As-Samīʿ heard you before you spoke, Al-Qarīb came close before you moved, and Al-Mujīb answered before you finished asking. Turn to the One whose door is open until the Day of Judgment — He commanded you to ask, and He promised to answer.

يَا سَمِيعُ

Inspire in me words pleasing to You. Make sincere the du'ā I utter between breaths. Let me never doubt that You are listening.

يَا قَرِيبُ

Let me feel Your nearness in the stillness of my prayer. Let its comfort cut through all the noise outside, and forgive me for what should have never made it inside.

يَا مُجِيبُ

Answer not only what I ask but what I mean. Give me what I would have prayed for if I knew what was best for me.

Who Protects You?

Names Covered

Al-Mu'min · Al-Muhaymin · Al-Ḥafīẓ · Al-Wakīl

Theme

Four walls of divine protection — inner security, surrounding oversight, preservation through trials, and complete trust

The Names

المُؤْمِنُ

Al-Mu'min

The Grantor of Security

المُهَيْمِنُ

Al-Muhaymin

The Overseeing Protector

الحَفِيظُ

Al-Ḥafīẓ

The Preserver

الوَكِيلُ

Al-Wakīl

The Ultimate Trustee

Background

Allah says: "By My might and My majesty, I will not combine upon My servant two fears or two securities." If you tremble here mindful of Him, you will be sheltered there. Two feelings of fear and two feelings of safety cannot live in the same heart — and with that balance, the doors of divine protection open.

Distinguishing the Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
المُؤْمِنُ Al-Mu'min Amn (safety) and īmān (faith) share the same root. He secures your outside from harm and your inside from doubt. "He who fed them when they were hungry and made them safe from fear."
المُهَيْمِنُ Al-Muhaymin If Al-Mu'min secures the inside, Al-Muhaymin protects the horizon. His watchfulness is not surveillance but security. When you're about to walk into harm's way, He redirects you.
الحَفِيظُ Al-Ḥafīẓ The third wall. He preserves the heavens from collapse, hearts from despair, and the Qur'an from corruption. "Be mindful of Allah and He will preserve you." Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said: "The Qur'an preserves him."
الوَكِيلُ Al-Wakīl Trusting Allah doesn't mean removing yourself — it means recognising He's the part that brings results. حَسْبِيَ اللَّهُ — He is sufficient for me. Ibn al-Qayyim: half the religion is turning to Allah, the other half is relying upon Him.

Key Points

PointDetail
Inner faith before outward rescue Hājar in the desert — before Zamzam, Allah put faith in her heart. At Badr — before the battle, He sent rain for purification, angels for fortification, and steadied their hearts. The inward reality always comes first.
Al-Ḥafīẓ guards across decades Ya'qūb said: فَاللَّهُ خَيْرٌ حَافِظًا. Allah wasn't only preserving Yūsuf — He was preserving Ya'qūb's heart, mind, and faith for twenty years. And He preserved Yūsuf's dream even inside his nightmare.
حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ Ibrāhīm said it mid-air into the fire. The Prophet ﷺ said it surrounded by an army. 'Ā'ishah said it under slander. Imām Mālik engraved it on his ring: "The very next āyah proves that deliverance always follows."

Takeaway & Du'ā

Al-Mu'min settles the heart. Al-Muhaymin surrounds you. Al-Ḥafīẓ preserves you. Al-Wakīl takes over when you can't see a way forward — but He always sees that way.

يَا مُؤْمِنُ

Grant me faith no fear can shake. Secure my heart in Your promise and let my trust in You outweigh every worry.

يَا مُهَيْمِنُ

Watch over me in moments no one else can see. Protect me from what I can and cannot see.

يَا حَفِيظُ

Preserve my faith through trial and time. Guard my loved ones when far from me, and protect me — for without You I cannot protect myself.

يَا وَكِيلُ

I entrust to You every matter. Take charge of what confuses me and make Your plan more beloved to me than my own.

Who Sees You?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Baṣīr · Ash-Shahīd · Ar-Raqīb · As-Sittīr

Theme

Being truly seen — divine sight that penetrates every darkness, witnessing that validates, watchfulness that awakens, and concealment that heals

The Names

البَصِيرُ

Al-Baṣīr

The All-Seeing

الشَّهِيدُ

Ash-Shahīd

The Ever-Present Witness

الرَّقِيبُ

Ar-Raqīb

The Ever-Watchful

السِّتِّيرُ

As-Sittīr

The Concealer

Background

It can feel like you're invisible and that no one seems to see your pain. But Allah says: "You are truly under Our watchful eyes." You are carried in His sights, not just caught by His sight. In a world where cameras capture your face but not your heart, His gaze is the only one that heals instead of hunts. People look for your low lights or maybe even your highlight reel. Al-Baṣīr looks at the hidden reel — the parts that you edit out even from yourself.

Distinguishing the Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
البَصِيرُ Al-Baṣīr He sees what no eye can penetrate. Yūnus (AS) was seen فِي ظُلْمَةِ اللَّيْلِ وَظُلْمَةِ الْبَحْرِ وَظُلْمَةِ بَطْنِ الْحُوتِ — through the darkness of night, sea, and whale. Your heart can feel like that dark ocean, and Al-Baṣīr sees right through it.
الشَّهِيدُ Ash-Shahīd Present in every scene, not just watching it — He testifies for you and to you. He witnesses your unseen labour: the patience, the swallowed pain, the held tongue that no one else noticed.
الرَّقِيبُ Ar-Raqīb The one who watches and accounts — He observes your thoughts before your actions. Al-Junayd said: "Allah's gaze upon you precedes your gaze upon whatever you're looking at."
السِّتِّيرُ As-Sittīr Al-Ghafūr covers the effects of sin; As-Sittīr covers the sin itself from people while calling your heart back to Him. Allah never exposes a person the first time — He gives them a chance to return.

Key Points

PointDetail
Iḥsān — seeing & being seen أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ — worship Him as if you see Him, and when you can't, know He sees you. Your greatest comfort here is worshipping Him while knowing He sees you; your greatest desire of the hereafter is seeing Him while knowing He's pleased with you.
Murāqabah interrupts sin at the root Ibn al-Jawzī maps the path: passing thought → settled thought → shaytān's pitch → intention → determination → action → habit. Practise murāqabah and you interrupt at the level of thought — before anything else follows. Al-Junayd said: "Allah's gaze upon you precedes your gaze upon whatever you're looking at."
Be the cloak, not the spotlight The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever covers the fault of a Muslim, Allah will cover him in this world and the hereafter." Sitr is not complicity — it's mercy that restrains the tongue while helping a person repent. The only exception: if they pose a threat to others.

Takeaway & Du'ā

When no one saw you, Al-Baṣīr saw you. When no one stood up for you, Ash-Shahīd recorded you. When you were about to derail, Ar-Raqīb awakened you. And when you couldn't bear the shame of your lapse into sin, As-Sittīr covered you.

يَا بَصِيرُ

You see what no one else sees. Every tear I hide, every effort unnoticed, every quiet act of good. Let me find comfort in being seen by You when I feel invisible to the rest of the world.

يَا شَهِيدُ

Be my witness when my intentions are pure and when they falter, let my fear of You set them aright.

يَا رَقِيبُ

Watch over my heart when it drifts and over my thoughts before they turn to deeds. Make me mindful of You in secret and in public until awareness of You becomes my protection from slip.

يَا سَتِّيرُ

Cover my faults with the same grace You always have. Hide my flaws even as You heal them. Let my repentance be quiet and my shame sincere and my forgiveness made complete beneath Your protective veil. Ameen.

Who Gives You Peace?

Watch

Names Covered

As-Subbūḥ · Al-Quddūs · As-Salām

Theme

Perfection, purity, and peace — declaring Him above all flaws, seeking purification from Him, and finding lasting calm in Him alone

The Names

السُّبُّوحُ

As-Subbūḥ

The Utterly Perfect

القُدُّوسُ

Al-Quddūs

The Entirely Pure

السَّلَامُ

As-Salām

The Source of Peace

Background

You weren't created for a life without storms — you were given what's needed to find stillness within them. The chaos of this world is meant to drive you to the source of peace, make you a source of peace, and make you long for the final destination of peace. Three names unfold inseparably: As-Subbūḥ, utterly perfect beyond all imperfection; Al-Quddūs, entirely pure who purifies what He wills; As-Salām, the one who is peace Himself and the source of any peace to be found within.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
السُّبُّوحُ As-Subbūḥ No flaw can ever be ascribed to Him. Sibāḥah means to swim — the swimmer is above the water — so you declare Him above all that is attributed to Him. Yūnus (AS) at the bottom of the ocean said: سُبْحَانَكَ — and had he not been among those who do Tasbīḥ, he would have remained in the belly of that whale until the Day of Judgment.
القُدُّوسُ Al-Quddūs No purity exists except from Him. He sanctifies places — Al-Quds is pure only by Al-Quddūs — and He sanctifies people through their deeds. Salman al-Fārisī said: "The land does not make anyone holy; only a person's deeds make them holy."
السَّلَامُ As-Salām He is peace, gives peace, and has an unlimited supply — because the one who doesn't have something can't give it. When we say "As-Salāmu 'alaykum" we're saying: no harm will come to you from me. On Laylat al-Qadr the angels descend with سَلَامٌ from the One who sent them.

Key Points

PointDetail
Tasbīḥ clears, Taqdīs fills Subbūḥ means no flaw can be ascribed to Him (intense negation). Quddūs means no purity exists except from Him (intense affirmation). Between them is the clearing of everything unworthy and the filling of every perfection. Think of the world as a deep ocean and Tasbīḥ as the only way to breathe in it.
Approach Al-Quddūs worthy When Mūsā approached the sacred valley, Allah said: "Remove your sandals" — come physically and spiritually pure. You purify outwardly through wuḍū' and inwardly through repentance: "O Allah, wash away my sins with water, snow, and hail." You could live in the holiest city and still have a corrupted heart if you don't pursue the One who made it holy.
From prayer mat to eternal home In Rukūʿ and Sujūd you say سُبُّوحٌ قُدُّوسٌ. At the close of prayer: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ — spreading the peace you just received. Then you seal it: اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ السَّلَامُ وَمِنْكَ السَّلَامُ — returning to the source. Those who know As-Salām don't just rest in peace when they die; they reflect it while alive.

Takeaway & Du'ā

Salām isn't just a greeting — it's the state you carry back from your prayer mat into a restless world before you return to your eternal home. Allah is calling you to Dār as-Salām, and He wants you to become peace so that when you finally enter, you won't be walking into it for the first time — you'll be returning to it.

يَا سُبُّوحُ

How perfect You are beyond everything I can imagine. When the world feels loud and heavy, remind me that You are untouched by its chaos. Let my praise of You rise above the darkness, even if I drown myself in it, and pull me out by Your perfection.

يَا قُدُّوسُ

Purify me inside and out. Cleanse my worship of pride, my heart of resentment, my soul of anything that clouds its way back to You, and make me worthy of the nearness I seek.

يَا سَلَامُ

Send peace into every storm within my chest. Let Your calm settle where fear once lived. Make me a source of peace for others until I meet You in the home of peace and hear from You Yourself: سَلَامٌ قَوْلًا مِّن رَّبٍّ رَّحِيمٍ. Ameen.

Who Fills Your Emptiness?

Al-Ghanī · Al-Ḥamīd · Al-Fattāḥ

Theme

Breaking free from cycles — finding contentment through the Self-Sufficient, grounding through the Praiseworthy, and new doors through the Opener

The Names

الغَنِيُّ

Al-Ghanī

The Self-Sufficient

الحَمِيدُ

Al-Ḥamīd

The Praiseworthy

الفَتَّاحُ

Al-Fattāḥ

The Opener

Background

Everything in this world moves in cycles — night and day, strength and weakness, gain and loss. Sometimes you get stuck inside one: the same sin, the same broken du'ā, the same goals set every Ramadan. Only Allah can reboot and reroute your life. In a hadith qudsī He says: "Devote yourself to My worship and I will fill your heart with richness and remove your poverty." Al-Ghanī and Al-Ḥamīd help you see what you already have; Al-Fattāḥ shows you what it can become.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
الغَنِيُّ Al-Ghanī Entirely free of need — not just wealthy but without need for your worship, obedience, thanks, or even your existence. يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ أَنتُمُ الْفُقَرَاءُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَاللَّهُ هُوَ الْغَنِيُّ الْحَمِيدُ. His love needs nothing from you, and that is the purest kind of love.
الحَمِيدُ Al-Ḥamīd Praiseworthy by His very essence, whether or not anyone praises Him. Thanks (shukr) is for a specific favour; praise (ḥamd) is for who the giver is. The more you praise a blessing, the more fulfilling it becomes — giving you richness in the heart and contentment through perspective.
الفَتَّاحُ Al-Fattāḥ The one who opens doors of repentance, comprehension, provision, and victory. مَّا يَفْتَحِ اللَّهُ لِلنَّاسِ مِن رَّحْمَةٍ فَلَا مُمْسِكَ لَهَا — whatever mercy He opens, none can withhold. Sometimes He opens the very door you're knocking on; sometimes He shows you a side door you never noticed.

Key Points

PointDetail
SubḥānAllāh rises, Alḥamdulillāh grounds When buried in hardship, "SubḥānAllāh" is how you rise above — like Yūnus. When blessed by ease, "Alḥamdulillāh" is how you stay grounded — like Sulaymān. One protects you from drowning in pain; the other protects you from being trapped by pleasure.
Don't beg from those as poor as you Wahb ibn Munabbih said: "Woe to you — you go to the one who shuts his door, shows you his poverty, and hides his wealth, and you leave the One who opens His door day and night, shows you His richness, and says: call upon Me, I will answer you."
Setbacks can be openings At Ḥudaybiyyah the Companions were turned away from the Ka'bah and bound by terms they didn't want. Then Allah revealed: إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُّبِينًا — what looked like defeat was the door to victory. Sometimes Al-Fattāḥ doesn't open a new door to a new reality, but an inner door to the same reality.

Takeaway & Du'ā

Al-Ghanī and Al-Ḥamīd remind you of what you have and can use with gratitude. Al-Fattāḥ shows you how to open what's next. If justice felt elusive here, know that no file goes missing on the Day of Judgment — the seals will be broken, the records opened, and every wrong met with His perfect fatḥ.

يَا غَنِيُّ

You are free of all need while I am always in need of You. Let me never beg from those as poor as me. Make me rich through contentment and fill my heart with gratitude instead of greed.

يَا حَمِيدُ

You are praised whether or not I remember to say it. Teach me to love You not just for what You give, but for who You are. Let my praise echo in hardship as it does in ease, and let every breath be a quiet thank You.

يَا فَتَّاحُ

Open doors I cannot find and hearts I cannot reach. Open paths to good where I only see walls, and open my chest to trust whatever You've decreed. Let every opening You grant lead me back to You. Ameen.

Ep 16

Who Controls Your Time?

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Qābiḍ · Al-Bāsiṭ · Al-Muqaddim · Al-Mu'akhkhir

Theme

Trusting divine timing — the One who withholds and expands, who advances and delays, all within His perfect wisdom

The Names

القَابِضُ

Al-Qābiḍ

The Withholder

البَاسِطُ

Al-Bāsiṭ

The Expander

المُقَدِّمُ

Al-Muqaddim

The Advancer

المُؤَخِّرُ

Al-Mu'akhkhir

The Delayer

Background

You curse time for moving too slow or too fast, forgetting He is the One turning it perfectly. Allah owns the pause, play, rewind, and forward buttons of your life. Both abundance and scarcity are tests. The tightening is not punishment — it is protection. The opening is not an invitation to indulgence — it is a test. And both come from the same hand. Qabd and Bast are the inhale and exhale of your soul — you can't live only inhaling, nor only exhaling.

Distinguishing the Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
القَابِضُ Al-Qābiḍ The One who tightens, draws in, and withholds. When He holds, He's not being stingy — He's teaching you to look inward and upward. Ṣabr during constriction becomes oxygen for Shukr during expansion.
البَاسِطُ Al-Bāsiṭ The One who opens, stretches out, and expands. When He gives, He's placing something in your hand that you can't let occupy your heart. Jibrīl squeezed the Prophet ﷺ until he thought he couldn't breathe — then Allah expanded his chest for the Qur'ān.
المُقَدِّمُ Al-Muqaddim He brings forward whom He wills when they are ready. Sometimes you're not being held back — you're being set up for something greater. He advances you only when the step will benefit you.
المُؤَخِّرُ Al-Mu'akhkhir He delays whom He wills to protect their soul. Yūsuf (AS) watched two cellmates leave prison before him — one to a palace, one to death — but when Allah finally brought him forward, it wasn't as a servant but as the ruler. What is delayed for you is not always denied.

Key Points

PointDetail
These names come in pairs Many scholars say Al-Qābiḍ and Al-Bāsiṭ should only be mentioned together — one without the other could suggest restricting without wisdom or giving without guidance. The same applies to Al-Muqaddim and Al-Mu'akhkhir. Both the squeeze and the release come from the same merciful hand.
The squeeze before the gift Jibrīl squeezed the Prophet ﷺ three times, preparing him for "a heavy word." Then Allah expanded his chest, removed his burden, and elevated his name. Mecca became unbearable, then Madīnah became his city, until Allah gave him back Mecca entirely. Allah squeezes, then He spreads — it's never outside His mercy.
Trust His pacing Umar ibn Abdul Aziz would pray: "O Allah, make me content with Your decree and bless me in what You destined for me, so I no longer wish to hasten what You delayed nor delay what You hastened." The real secret of peace: not when things finally happen, but when you finally stop wishing they would.

Takeaway & Du'ā

He owns the remote control — and that is His mercy, because you would have fast-forwarded through the very scene He wrote to prepare you for the ending you begged for. Time stops feeling like an enemy and starts feeling like a teacher when you trust the Withholder in hardship, the Expander in ease, and the Delayer when He slows you down.

يَا قَابِضُ

When You withhold, let me not lose hope. Tighten my path only to redirect me to Yours. Teach me to find You in the squeeze and to trust that every contraction is preparation for what comes next.

يَا بَاسِطُ

When You expand, let me not lose humility. Widen my provision in ways that only increase my gratitude to You, and never let what You place in my hand occupy my heart.

يَا مُقَدِّمُ

When You bring me forward, make me worthy of the moment. Advance me only when the step will please You, and let every opening You grant draw me closer to You.

يَا مُؤَخِّرُ

When You hold me back, make me patient with the waiting. Delay me only when the pause will protect me. Wherever I stand in Your decree, let me feel content that I am never misplaced. Ameen.

Ep 17

The Wisdom Behind Your Pain

Watch

Names Covered

Al-'Alīm · Al-Ḥakīm · Al-Laṭīf · Al-Khabīr

Theme

Trusting the plan you can't see — the One who knows all, decrees with wisdom, works gently in the details, and is fully aware of what's within you

The Names

العَلِيمُ

Al-'Alīm

The All-Knowing

الحَكِيمُ

Al-Ḥakīm

The All-Wise

اللَّطِيفُ

Al-Laṭīf

The Ever-Subtle, Gentle, Kind

الخَبِيرُ

Al-Khabīr

The All-Aware

Background

When pain feels pointless, remember Ya'qūb's advice to Yūsuf before he was ripped away: "Surely your Lord is All-Knowing, All-Wise" (Yūsuf 12:6). Al-'Alīm knows all the whats. Al-Ḥakīm knows all the whys. Al-Laṭīf delivers His care through hidden paths. Al-Khabīr sees what no one else can — inside and out. If you internalize this one āyah, you'll know how to interpret everything in life that you can't interpret.

Distinguishing the Four Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
العَلِيمُ Al-'Alīm He who knows it's going to happen before it does. He knows what was, what is, what will be, and exactly how it would have been otherwise — all the near-infinite alternative histories of your life.
الحَكِيمُ Al-Ḥakīm He who allows it to happen anyway, for a greater purpose. Almost always paired with Al-'Alīm — together they held Yūsuf through the well, the palace, and the prison cell.
اللَّطِيفُ Al-Laṭīf The Subtle, Gentle, and Kind — all at once. Two strands: the hidden details only He knows, and the hidden ways He delivers benefits. You often only recognize luṭf in hindsight. "Allah sends down rain and then the earth becomes green. Surely Allah is Most Subtle, All-Aware" (Al-Ḥajj 22:63).
الخَبِيرُ Al-Khabīr He who knows the inside and outside, cause and consequence. Even the angels only record deeds — Al-Khabīr records hidden intentions. He has full awareness of you and of the one who intends you harm — but always with mercy.

Key Points

PointDetail
Ya'qūb and Yūsuf carried these names like lamps When his children called him a crazy old man, Ya'qūb responded: "I know from Allah what you do not know" (Yūsuf 12:86) — meaning: I know Him to be 'Alīm and Ḥakīm. Yūsuf held those same names through the well and prison. When the story curved back and he stood as ruler of Egypt, he unveiled a name you can only taste after the storm: "My Lord is Laṭīf in what He wills" (Yūsuf 12:100).
Luqmān pairs Laṭīf and Khabīr "Even if a deed were the weight of a mustard seed, hidden in a stone or lost in the heavens and the earth, Allah will bring it forward. Surely Allah is Laṭīf, Khabīr" (Luqmān 31:16). Laṭīf finds the buried seed; Khabīr brings it forth with precision. The smallest obedience and quietest treachery both have weight.
Don't put Allah's plan on trial Hold yourself accountable to your deeds, not Allah accountable to your understanding of His decree. The angels said: "We have no knowledge except what You taught us. You are Al-'Alīm, Al-Ḥakīm" (Al-Baqarah 2:32). When you say "I don't see the wisdom," answer yourself: "Of course I don't — His wisdom comes from knowledge I can't encompass."

Takeaway & Du'ā

You're not the writer or the reader of your story — you're the book being authored. When you can say "I know that He knows, and I know that He knows why," that's enough.

يَا عَلِيمُ

You know what I hide and what I show, what I've done and what I will do. Let what You know of me be better than what people know of me, and forgive me for what only You know. Teach me what benefits me, and let what You keep hidden from me always be for my good.

يَا حَكِيمُ

Your wisdom surrounds what I cannot understand. When I question the purpose, remind me of Your perfection. Grant me the patience to trust Your timing and the insight to see the beauty unfolding in Your decree.

يَا لَطِيفُ

Work gently through the details of my life. Send kindnesses that I may not notice until I look back, but can still feel at every moment. Let Your subtle care reach the corners of my heart that no words or people could ever touch.

يَا خَبِيرُ

You are fully aware of what's within me — my fears, my flaws, and my hidden intentions. Purify what You see inside of me until it is pleasing to You. And let nothing inside me contradict what will be produced when I stand before You bare. Ameen.

Ep 18

Why Allah Delays the Fall of Tyrants

Watch

Names Covered

Al-Qahhār · Al-Jabbār · Al-Mutakabbir

Theme

Names that are beautiful only for Allah — the One who subdues tyrants, mends the broken, and whose pride is pure justice, not arrogance

The Names

القَهَّارُ

Al-Qahhār

The Subduer, The Overpowering

الجَبَّارُ

Al-Jabbār

The Compeller, The Healer

المُتَكَبِّرُ

Al-Mutakabbir

The Supremely Proud

Background

Sometimes it isn't your own sin that breaks you — it's someone else's perceived power. Not every name of Allah is meant to be mirrored by us. Some are beautiful only for Him and disastrous when humans claim them. Al-Qahhār subdues the tyrant, Al-Jabbār mends the victim, and Al-Mutakabbir's pride is pure justice — never arrogance.

Distinguishing the Three Names

NameTransliterationMeaning
القَهَّارُ Al-Qahhār The One with the upper hand over all creation. He governs your heartbeat without permission and controls the tyrant's next breath without asking. Al-Qahhār presses down and crushes those who deserve humiliation.
الجَبَّارُ Al-Jabbār For the tyrant: the Compeller who subjugates. For the victim: the Healer. Jabr means to set a broken bone — painful but healing. Al-Qahhār breaks; Al-Jabbār repairs. "Algebra" comes from jabr — fixing broken equations. Only Allah has the equation to your soul.
المُتَكَبِّرُ Al-Mutakabbir His pride offers safety, not insecurity. Too great to wrong anyone, too majestic to be petty. When He humiliates the arrogant, it's justice. When He delays a tyrant's fall, it's precision. "Whoever humbles himself for Allah, Allah exalts him; whoever exalts himself, Allah lowers him."

Key Points

PointDetail
His delay is not neglect "Do not think that Allah is unaware of what the oppressors do. He only delays them to a day when their eyes will stare in horror" (Ibrāhīm 14:42). Allah speaks directly to the victim — comforting you. He mocks the schemers and promises He will not fail to avenge you.
Every tyrant is toppled by something smaller Fir'awn drowned in the water he claimed to control — by the staff of Mūsā. Nimrūd was taken by a mosquito. Every tyrant falls to something smaller so he can see how small he always was. Even before death, Al-Qahhār destroys them from within — no peace of mind, no trust, no real love.
Majestic for Him, disastrous for us When humans play Qahhār, they become manipulators. When they play Jabbār, tyrants. When they act Mutakabbir, they shrink. "Glory is My lower garment and pride is My cloak. Whoever competes with Me in either, I will punish him." Check for the micro-tyrannies inside you — every Fir'awn started small.

Takeaway & Du'ā

When your oppressor mocks you, call upon Al-Qahhār. When you're broken, call upon Al-Jabbār. When egos destroy, seek refuge in Al-Mutakabbir. But before you rage against the tyrant "out there," make sure you don't have a little Fir'awn inside yourself.

يَا قَهَّارُ

Subdue every tyrant who raises a hand against the innocent. Break the arrogance of those who spill the blood of Your beloved servants. Let their cruelty be crushed by Your justice. Subdue also the arrogance within me, so that I never resemble the oppressor in any form.

يَا جَبَّارُ

Mend what their hands have broken. Rebuild the lands they've shattered and heal the hearts that tremble for You under their bombs and boots. Set right the fractures in our ummah, and make every wound a doorway back to You. When You allow us to break, let it only be to rebuild us stronger.

يَا مُتَكَبِّرُ

You are too glorious for injustice to prevail, too mighty for oppression to last before You. Show the tyrants that no throne stands forever. Honor the humble who still call Your name beneath the rubble. Clothe our people in dignity when the world strips them bare, and raise them in rank and protect them from despair. Ameen.
