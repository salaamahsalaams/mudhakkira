Ep 01

This Destroys Every Problem

Watch

Dhikr

Lā Ḥawla wa Lā Quwwata illā Billāh

Theme

Surrender, transformation & reliance on Allah alone

The Dhikr

لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh

There is no transformation or power except through Allah

Note: Ḥawl derives from taḥwīl — transformation, the shift from one state (ḥāl) to another. The phrase means: no change of state, and no strength to do anything, is possible except through Allah.

Background

The Prophet ﷺ called it "a treasure from the treasures of Paradise" and taught it to 19 companions by name. It is one of the five remaining righteous deeds alongside SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illAllah, and Allahu Akbar.

When & Why to Say It

PointDetail
It declares Allah as your only source of help Just as La ilaha illAllah makes Him the only object of worship, this phrase makes Him the only source of help. When you say it, Allah responds: "My servant has submitted and surrendered."
Say it at the adhan, when leaving home, and when overwhelmed Respond to "come to prayer" with it — admitting you cannot move from heedlessness to worship without Him. Say it with Bismillah when leaving home for guidance and protection. Say it when overwhelmed, exhausted, or struck by hardship.
It conquers sin and protects from the evil eye Ibn Taymiyyah said: "Let his constant refuge be this phrase — through it, terrors are met, burdens are carried, and lofty stations are reached." Say Masha'Allah la quwwata illa billah when you see a blessing — to protect it.

Takeaway

Every transformation — from sickness to health, from sin to obedience — comes only from Allah. Say it when overwhelmed, exhausted, tempted, or struck by news. It is an act of surrender that invites His help.

Ep 02

The Du'ā of Yūnus (AS)

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā of Yūnus — 3 phrases

Theme

Distress, Tawḥīd, glorification & confession as the path to relief

The Dhikr

Arabic Transliteration Meaning
لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ Lā ilāha illā anta There is nothing worthy of worship except You
سُبْحَانَكَ Subḥānaka Exalted are You — free from all imperfection
إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ Innī kuntu mina al-ẓālimīn Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers

Background

Yunus (AS) left his people after years of rejection — without Allah's permission. He boarded a ship, lots were drawn three times and each time fell on him. Thrown overboard and swallowed by a whale, in total darkness he prostrated and made this du'a. Allah saved him.

What Makes This Du'a Work

ElementExplanation
It combines four things at once Tawhid (only Allah can save you), glorification (You are perfect — what happened is not injustice from You), servitude (I am dependent on You), and confession (I wronged myself). This combination is the most direct path to relief.
The promise is absolute The Prophet ﷺ said: "No Muslim supplicates with it for anything ever except that Allah responds." Not sometimes — always. (Al-Tirmidhi)
Everyone has a whale Fear, anxiety, addiction, negligence — whatever has swallowed you. Allah said He saved Yunus "and that is how We save the believers" — in every time and place (21:88).

Takeaway

Say these three phrases when you feel trapped, hopeless, or stuck. You are declaring Tawḥīd, exalting Allah, acknowledging your need, and confessing your shortcomings — the most direct path to relief from any hardship.

Ep 03

The Du'ā to Say at Iftar

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā at Breaking the Fast — 3 phrases

Theme

Gratitude, humility & the principle that hardship passes but reward remains

The Dhikr

Arabic Transliteration Meaning
ذَهَبَ الظَّمَأُ Dhahaba al-ẓama'u The thirst is gone
وَابْتَلَّتِ الْعُرُوقُ Wabtallatil-'urūqu The veins are moistened
وَثَبَتَ الْأَجْرُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ Wa thabata al-ajru inshā'Allāh And the reward is confirmed, if Allah wills

Source: Narrated by Ibn ʿUmar (RA), reported by Abu Dawud.

Background

Say this before you eat at iftar. The thirst passed, the body is being restored, and you hope the reward was earned. The "insha'Allah" at the end is humility — you completed the action, but only Allah knows if He accepted it.

Breaking It Down

PointDetail
Why "insha'Allah" for the reward You don't know if your fast was spiritually intact. The Prophet ﷺ said: "How many a person fasts and gets nothing from their fast except hunger and thirst" — because they violated it with backbiting, lying, or insincerity.
Hardship of worship is temporary, its reward is permanent The thirst passes within minutes of iftar. But the reward remains. Sins work the opposite way — the pleasure is temporary, the consequences are lasting. This du'a puts that reality into words.
Make du'a throughout the entire fast The fasting person has an accepted du'a throughout the fast, not just at iftar. Don't wait for the last moments — call on Allah while driving, sitting, or waiting. Be present at iftar and say this du'a before reaching for food.

Takeaway

This du'ā is the spiritual close to your day of fasting. Say it before you reach for food — not as a habit, but as a genuine moment of acknowledgement before Allah.

Ep 04

This Du'ā Covers Everything You Could Need

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā al-Qunūt — the Witr supplication

Theme

Six comprehensive requests: guidance, wellness, Allah's friendship, baraka, protection & praise

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ اهْدِنِي فِيمَنْ هَدَيْتَ وَعَافِنِي فِيمَنْ عَافَيْتَ وَتَوَلَّنِي فِيمَنْ تَوَلَّيْتَ وَبَارِكْ لِي فِيمَا أَعْطَيْتَ وَقِنِي شَرَّ مَا قَضَيْتَ فَإِنَّكَ تَقْضِي وَلَا يُقْضَى عَلَيْكَ وَإِنَّهُ لَا يَذِلُّ مَنْ وَالَيْتَ وَلَا يَعِزُّ مَنْ عَادَيْتَ تَبَارَكْتَ رَبَّنَا وَتَعَالَيْتَ

Allāhumma ihdinī fīman hadayt, wa ʿāfinī fīman ʿāfayt, wa tawallanī fīman tawallayt, wa bārik lī fīmā aʿṭayt, wa qinī sharra mā qaḍayt. Fa-innaka taqḍī wa lā yuqḍā ʿalayk. Wa innahū lā yadhillu man wālayt, wa lā yaʿizzu man ʿādayt. Tabārakta rabbanā wa taʿālayt.

O Allah, guide me amongst those You've guided; give me wellness amongst those You've given wellness to; protect me among those You've protected; bless me in what has been given to me by You; and protect me from any evil You've decreed. For You decree in truth and none decree over You. None can honour who You oppose, and none can humiliate who You support. Blessed are You, our Lord, and Exalted.

Source: Narrated by al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī (RA), reported by Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhī.

Background

The Prophet ﷺ personally taught this du'a to his grandson al-Hasan ibn Ali (RA) — who was under 10 years old — for his Witr prayer. This is the only sunnah-legislated du'a for Witr. Six requests, less than 30 seconds to say.

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PhraseWhat It Means
Guide me Guidance has multiple levels — to good deeds, to acting on what you know, to better actions, to consistency, and to die upon goodness. You need all of them at every stage of life.
Give me wellness Comprehensive well-being — from disease, from bad character, from innovation, from misguidance. Not just the body; the heart and religion too.
Befriend me Asking Allah to take charge of your affairs and not leave you to yourself — even for a moment. The believers are the allies of Allah.
Bless me in what You have given Baraka is quality over quantity. Some live 100 years and accomplish nothing; others leave a legacy at 20. Make my limited time and resources go far.
Protect me from the evil You've decreed Evil is in creation, not in Allah's action. When rain causes a flood, the evil is in the circumstance — not in Allah decreeing it. You're asking Him to turn that evil away from you.
For You decree and none decrees over You This is the reasoning behind the request. No one can reverse Your judgment. Nothing You said will happen can be made not to happen. Nothing in creation can obligate You.
None is humiliated whom You support Physical harm is not humiliation — humiliation is internal, a degradation of the soul that comes only from disobedience. Whoever Allah supports is honoured regardless of what happens to the body.
None is honoured whom You oppose Whoever Allah takes as an enemy is lowly — even if the world celebrates them with wealth, power, and fame. True honour belongs only to the people of faith.
Blessed are You, our Lord, and Exalted You close with praise, not another request. You are the source of all goodness and You are exalted above all. The du'a begins with your need and closes with His greatness.

Takeaway

This is the only sunnah-legislated du'ā for the Witr prayer — short, comprehensive, and profound. Say it every Witr, not just in Ramadan.

Ep 05

The Master of Seeking Forgiveness

Watch

Dhikr

Sayyid al-Istighfār — Master of Seeking Forgiveness

Theme

Complete framework of repentance — Lordship, servitude, covenant, confession & forgiveness

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ رَبِّي لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ خَلَقْتَنِي وَأَنَا عَبْدُكَ وَأَنَا عَلَى عَهْدِكَ وَوَعْدِكَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُ أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا صَنَعْتُ أَبُوءُ لَكَ بِنِعْمَتِكَ عَلَيَّ وَأَبُوءُ بِذَنْبِي فَاغْفِرْ لِي فَإِنَّهُ لَا يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ

Allāhumma anta rabbī, lā ilāha illā anta, khalaqtanī wa anā ʿabduka, wa anā ʿalā ʿahdika wa waʿdika mastaṭaʿt. Aʿūdhu bika min sharri mā ṣanaʿt. Abū'u laka biniʿmatika ʿalayya wa abū'u bidhanbī. Faghfirlī fa-innahū lā yaghfiru al-dhunūba illā ant.

O Allah, You are my Lord. There is no deity worthy of worship except You. You created me and I am Your servant, and I am faithful to my covenant and promise to You as much as I can. I seek refuge in You from the evil of what I have done. I acknowledge Your favour upon me, and I acknowledge my sin. So forgive me — for indeed none forgives sins except You.

Source: Narrated by Shaddād ibn Aws (RA), Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (7393).

Background

The Prophet ﷺ called this Sayyid al-Istighfār — the master of all forms of seeking forgiveness. It is not just "sorry" or "forgive me." It is a complete picture of your relationship with Allah: who He is, who you are, what He has given you, and what you have done in return. Whoever says it during the day with firm faith and dies before evening, or says it at night and dies before morning, is promised paradise.

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PhraseWhat It Means
You are my Lord Starting with acknowledgement — He sustains you, manages your life, provides and protects. He is running things, not you.
No deity except You Moving from Lordship to worship — since You are my creator and provider, You alone deserve my worship. Not career, not status, not desires.
I am Your servant Declaring your position honestly — not equal, not independent. Every person, regardless of title or achievement, says: "I am nothing but Your servant."
I am faithful to my covenant as much as I can Humility — not "I'm doing well," but "I'm trying with my weak capacity." Without this phrase, you'd be lying. None of us can fulfil Allah's rights the way they deserve.
I seek refuge in You from the evil of what I have done Running TO Allah because of your sins — not away from Him. You're not fleeing from Allah; you're fleeing to Him. Where else would you go?
I acknowledge Your favour and I acknowledge my sin From You is goodness and every blessing; from me is sin and wrongdoing. When you truly internalise this, arrogance becomes impossible. You are the one who keeps falling short and Allah is the one who keeps giving anyway.
So forgive me, for none forgives sins except You The request comes only at the end — after acknowledging His lordship, His right to be worshipped, His blessings, and your own sins. That sequence is deliberate. You close with certainty: no saint, no scholar, no intermediary — only He forgives.

Takeaway

Say it every morning and every evening — not as a habit, but as a moment of standing before Allah with your full reality. It is not just seeking forgiveness; it is a complete renewal of your relationship with Him.

Ep 06

This Du'ā Feeds Your Soul When It's Starving

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā for Anxiety & Grief

Theme

Removing worry and sadness by connecting to the Quran

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي عَبْدُكَ ابْنُ عَبْدِكَ ابْنُ أَمَتِكَ نَاصِيَتِي بِيَدِكَ مَاضٍ فِيَّ حُكْمُكَ عَدْلٌ فِيَّ قَضَاؤُكَ أَسْأَلُكَ بِكُلِّ اسْمٍ هُوَ لَكَ سَمَّيْتَ بِهِ نَفْسَكَ أَوْ عَلَّمْتَهُ أَحَدًا مِنْ خَلْقِكَ أَوْ أَنْزَلْتَهُ فِي كِتَابِكَ أَوِ اسْتَأْثَرْتَ بِهِ فِي عِلْمِ الْغَيْبِ عِنْدَكَ أَنْ تَجْعَلَ الْقُرْآنَ رَبِيعَ قَلْبِي وَنُورَ صَدْرِي وَجَلَاءَ حُزْنِي وَذَهَابَ هَمِّي

Allāhumma innī ʿabduka ibnu ʿabdika ibnu amatika, nāṣiyatī biyadika, māḍin fiyya ḥukmuka, ʿadlun fiyya qaḍā'uka. As'aluka bikulli ismin huwa laka, sammayta bihi nafsaka, aw ʿallamtahu aḥadan min khalqika, aw anzaltahu fī kitābika, aw ista'tharta bihi fī ʿilm il-ghaybi ʿindaka, an tajʿal al-Qur'āna rabīʿa qalbī, wa nūra ṣadrī, wa jalā'a ḥuznī, wa dhahāba hammī.

O Allah, I am Your servant, son of Your servant, son of Your maidservant. My forelock is in Your hand. Your command over me is forever executed and Your decree over me is just. I ask You by every name that You have — which You have named Yourself, or taught to any of Your creation, or revealed in any of Your books, or kept hidden in the knowledge of the unseen with You — that You make the Quran the spring of my heart, the light of my chest, the remover of my sadness, and the repeller of my stress.

Source: Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (RA), reported by Aḥmad.

Background

The Prophet ﷺ said: "No one is ever afflicted with worry or grief and says this except that Allah will remove his worry and grief and replace it with joy." The companions asked: "Should we learn it?" He said: "Yes, everyone who hears it should learn it."

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PhraseWhat It Means
I am Your servant, son of Your servant You start with total humility — your entire lineage belongs to Allah. You, your father, your mother — all servants of His.
My forelock is in Your hand You have no power, no control, no ability except through Him. He is in complete control of you. When you internalise that, your heart finds rest — you stop fighting reality.
Your command over me is forever executed Whatever Allah has decreed will happen. What missed you could never have reached you and what reached you could never have missed you.
Your decree over me is just There is no injustice in it — even the sadness you are feeling right now. Whatever happened to cause it, Allah is just in His decree.
I ask You by every name that You have You invoke every name of Allah — those He revealed in His books, those He taught to any of His creation, and those He kept hidden in the unseen. Not one name, not ten — all of them.
Make the Quran the spring of my heart Spring brings life after a barren winter. You are asking Allah to make the Quran do that for your heart — bring it back to life. This is not just a du'a to remove anxiety; it is a du'a to connect you to the Quran so the Quran removes it. The medicine is the Quran — but you have to actually read it.

Takeaway

Your body is nourished by food and water, but your soul is nourished by Quran, prayer, and dhikr. Whoever turns away from Allah's remembrance will have a constricted life. Memorise this du'a, say it when worry or grief strikes — and pair it with a daily portion of the Quran.

Ep 07

Don't Leave Home Without This

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā When Leaving the House — 3 phrases

Theme

Tawḥīd, tawakkul & spiritual armour before stepping into the world

The Dhikr

Arabic Transliteration Meaning
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ Bismillāh In the name of Allah
تَوَكَّلْتُ عَلَى اللَّهِ Tawakkaltu ʿalā Allāh I place my trust in Allah
وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ Wa lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh There is no transformation or power except through Allah

Source: Narrated by Anas ibn Mālik (RA), reported by Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhī.

Background

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever says these three phrases when leaving their house is told: "You have been guided, you have been sufficed, and you have been protected" — and Shayṭān turns away from them. Three sentences, spoken in seconds, and the devils outside your door give up on you.

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PointDetail
Each phrase earns a specific protection Bismillāh earns guidance — Allah directs you in your religious and worldly affairs. Tawakkul earns sufficiency — whoever relies upon Allah, He is enough for them. Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata earns protection — you declared Him your source of power, so now you are shielded by the greatest source of power. Three phrases, three results, perfectly aligned.
Tawakkul transforms how you live, not just how you leave Someone with true tawakkul does not cheat when the boss is absent or cut corners when no one is watching. They are not working for people's approval or fearing people's punishment — they are working for Allah. Allah didn't say whoever relies upon Him will be rewarded; He said He is sufficient for them. That is the reward, because sufficiency is what you wanted when you relied on Him.
Shayṭān gives up on you The devils wait outside your door every day to mislead you. When you say these words, Shayṭān turns away and says to another: "How can you deal with a person who has been guided, sufficed, and protected?" You built a fortress around yourself with three sentences. Think about all the times you grabbed your keys and left without saying it — no du'ā, no tawakkul, no armour.

Takeaway

These three sentences take seconds to say. They are your spiritual armour — guidance, sufficiency, and protection — before you step into a world where anything could happen. Say them every single time you leave your house.

Ep 08

The Perfect Way to Enter Salah

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā al-Istiftāḥ — Opening supplication of Salāh

Theme

Entering prayer cleansed & conscious of your need for Allah's mercy

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ بَاعِدْ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَ خَطَايَايَ كَمَا بَاعَدْتَ بَيْنَ الْمَشْرِقِ وَالْمَغْرِبِ ۞ اللَّهُمَّ نَقِّنِي مِنْ خَطَايَايَ كَمَا يُنَقَّى الثَّوْبُ الْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ الدَّنَسِ ۞ اللَّهُمَّ اغْسِلْنِي مِنْ خَطَايَايَ بِالثَّلْجِ وَالْمَاءِ وَالْبَرَدِ

Allāhumma bāʿid baynī wa bayna khaṭāyāya kamā bāʿadta bayna al-mashriqi wa al-maghrib. Allāhumma naqqinī min khaṭāyāya kamā yunaqqā al-thawbu al-abyaḍu min al-danas. Allāhumma ghsilnī min khaṭāyāya bi al-thalji wa al-mā'i wa al-barad.

O Allah, distance me from my sins as You have distanced the East from the West. O Allah, cleanse me of my sins as a white garment is cleansed of filth. O Allah, wash me of my sins with snow, water, and hail.

Source: Narrated by Abū Hurayrah (RA), reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

Background

The Prophet ﷺ would pause briefly after the opening takbīr and before reciting al-Fātiḥah. Abū Hurayrah noticed his beard moving slightly during this pause and asked what he was saying — so the Prophet ﷺ taught him this du'ā. It is called du'ā al-istiftāḥ, the opening supplication of prayer.

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PointDetail
Distance is sought from both sides The word bāʿid comes in a form indicating intensity and mutuality — you are asking Allah to distance you from your sins and your sins from you. No proximity from either direction. The level of separation you request is the distance between the east and the west, the furthest imaginable, with no possibility of overlap.
Three requests that cover every kind of cleansing First, distance me from my sins — remove them far away. Second, purify me — like stains removed from a white garment, where even the smallest mark is visible. Third, wash me with snow, water, and hail — because the scholars say sins carry heat and stickiness, causing stress and anxiety, and you are asking Allah to cool your heart and strip away every trace.
This du'ā is designed for the one who feels unworthy to pray You say this before al-Fātiḥah, before bowing, before prostration. Many people feel so ashamed of their sins that they cannot bring themselves to stand before Allah. This du'ā becomes the entrance — it teaches you to say: whatever sins I carry, however broken I am, distance me from them and cleanse me. It sets the tone for your entire prayer and helps your heart stay present.

Takeaway

This du'ā is said in the brief pause between the opening takbīr and al-Fātiḥah. Learn it, memorise it, and make it your habit the way it was his ﷺ habit. When you start your prayer by asking to be cleansed, you remind yourself why you are standing there — a sinful servant in need of a merciful Lord.

Ep 09

The Evil Eye Is Real

Watch

Dhikr

Invoking barakah — اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لَهُ / لَهَا when you admire something

Theme

The evil eye is real — how to protect yourself and others from it

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لَهُ ۞ اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لَهَا

Allāhumma bārik lahu · Allāhumma bārik lahā

O Allah, bless him with it. · O Allah, bless her with it.

Source: The Prophet ﷺ said: "If you see something you admire in your brother, make du'ā for barakah for them." — reported by Ibn Mājah and others. Also: مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ · تَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Background

The companion Sahl ibn Ḥunayf went to bathe, and ʿĀmir ibn Rabīʿah looked at him and remarked on his flawless skin. Sahl immediately collapsed. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Why would one of you kill his brother? If you see something you admire, make du'ā for barakah for them." He then commanded ʿĀmir to wash his face, hands, elbows, knees, and a portion of his stomach in a container of water, and that water was poured over Sahl — who got up completely fine.

Breaking Down the Topic

PointDetail
The evil eye is real, not folklore The Prophet ﷺ said: "al-ʿayn ḥaqq" — the evil eye is real. It can cause loss of health and wealth. It is connected to the soul — when someone sees something they admire and their soul is drawn to it, harm can occur. The mechanics are from the unseen; Allah knows how it works.
Admiration, not just envy You do not need to envy someone to give them the evil eye. ʿĀmir did not envy Sahl — he was a righteous companion who simply admired him. You can give evil eye to yourself and even to your own children. Any admiration without invoking Allah's blessings can become harmful.
The prophetic cure If someone has been afflicted, the one suspected of casting the eye is asked to wash their face, hands up to the elbows, knees, and a portion of their stomach in a container of water. That water is then poured over the afflicted person. The Prophet ﷺ said: "If you are asked to bathe for your brother, then do it."
The healing du'ā of the Prophet ﷺ When the Prophet ﷺ came to Sahl, he struck his chest and said: "Allāhumma adh-hib ʿanhu ḥarrahā wa bardahā wa waṣabahā" — O Allah, remove from him its heat, its cold, and its pain. Sahl recovered completely, as reported by Aḥmad and Ibn Mājah.
Avoid the two extremes The first extreme is denying the evil eye altogether — this contradicts authentic prophetic text. The second is obsession — blaming every flat tyre, every cold, and every bad grade on the evil eye, hiding your blessings and fabricating problems so no one envies you. Allah says: "As for the favour of your Lord, proclaim it." The balanced approach is acknowledging Allah's blessings openly while making du'ā for barakah when you admire something.

Takeaway

When you see something you admire — in yourself or in others — say Allāhumma bārik immediately. Do not hide your blessings out of fear; acknowledge them with gratitude and protect them with dhikr. The evil eye is real, but so is the protection Allah taught us.

Ep 10

The Gift You Say in Every Prayer

Watch

Dhikr

Aṣ-Ṣalāh al-Ibrāhīmiyyah — sending ṣalāh upon the Prophet ﷺ

Theme

The formula of sending blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ — taught by him, recited at least ten times daily in prayer

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ كَمَا صَلَّيْتَ عَلَى إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَعَلَى آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ ۞ اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ كَمَا بَارَكْتَ عَلَى آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ

Allāhumma ṣalli ʿalā Muḥammadin wa ʿalā āli Muḥammad, kamā ṣallayta ʿalā Ibrāhīma wa ʿalā āli Ibrāhīm, innaka Ḥamīdun Majīd. Allāhumma bārik ʿalā Muḥammadin wa ʿalā āli Muḥammad, kamā bārakta ʿalā āli Ibrāhīm, innaka Ḥamīdun Majīd.

O Allah, send Your blessings upon Muḥammad and upon the family of Muḥammad, as You sent blessings upon Ibrāhīm and upon the family of Ibrāhīm. Indeed, You are Praiseworthy and Glorious. O Allah, bless Muḥammad and the family of Muḥammad, as You blessed the family of Ibrāhīm. Indeed, You are Praiseworthy and Glorious.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim — narrated by Ka'b ibn 'Ujrah (RA). Also narrated in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī from the hadith of Ka'b ibn 'Ujrah who said: "Shall I not give you a gift that I heard from Rasūlullāh ﷺ himself?"

Background

When the verse was revealed in Sūrah al-Aḥzāb — "Indeed, Allah confers His blessings upon the Prophet, and His angels do so too. O you who believe, ask Allah to confer blessings upon him and grant him peace" — the companions already knew how to give salām from the tashahhud. But how do you send ṣalāh? A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked. He went silent — so long that the companions wished the question had never been asked. Then he gave them the formula we recite in every single prayer: the Ṣalāh al-Ibrāhīmiyyah.

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PointDetail
What does ṣalāh mean depending on who does it? When Allah sends ṣalāh upon someone, He mentions that person in the highest assembly among the angels — it is divine honour and praise. When the angels send ṣalāh, they ask Allah for forgiveness and mercy for that person. When we send ṣalāh upon the Prophet ﷺ, we are making du'ā — asking Allah to bestow His mercy and honour upon Rasūlullāh ﷺ. Three different meanings, same word, all revolving around honour, mercy, and elevation.
Who is the āl of Muḥammad? Three levels: (1) The household upon whom charity is forbidden — the descendants of Banū Hāshim and ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, including the families of ʿAlī, Jaʿfar, al-ʿAbbās, and ʿAqīl. (2) In another narration the Prophet ﷺ included "his wives and his descendants" — Khadījah, ʿĀ'ishah, Fāṭimah, al-Ḥasan, and al-Ḥusayn. (3) All followers of Rasūlullāh ﷺ — the entire ummah.
Why compare to Ibrāhīm's family? The comparison is not between the two prophets — Rasūlullāh ﷺ is the greatest. It's between the two ummahs. Ibrāhīm's followers include Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb, Ismāʿīl, Yūsuf, Mūsā, Hārūn, Dāwūd, Sulaymān, ʿĪsā, Zakariyyā, and Yaḥyā — an all-star team of prophets. On this side: one prophet ﷺ and the rest are civilians — us. You're asking Allah to give this ummah with only one prophet the same weight, barakah, and mercy as that ummah filled with prophets.
Why end with Al-Ḥamīd Al-Majīd? Al-Ḥamīd — the most praiseworthy. Every act of praise ever uttered, every tongue that has ever glorified, every heart that has ever been grateful, it all points back to Allah. He is inherently worthy of praise whether praised or not. Al-Majīd — the most glorious, exalted in His essence, attributes, and actions, with no limits to His majesty. We close by acknowledging that He alone is worthy of such a request and has the power to grant it.

Takeaway

You say this du'ā at minimum ten times a day in your five daily prayers. It was a gift from the Prophet ﷺ himself — one he paused and reflected before giving. Be present as you recite it, contemplate its meanings, and know that through it you are connecting yourself to the greatest chain of love and honour in existence. May Allah send His ṣalāh upon Rasūlullāh ﷺ, upon his family, and upon all of us trying our best to walk his path.

The Du'ā Before Entering the Bathroom

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā for entering and leaving the bathroom — seeking refuge from evil and evildoers

Theme

Spiritual protection in the most vulnerable place — the bathroom is where shayṭān is most active and dhikr is prohibited

The Dhikr

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ ، اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْخُبُثِ وَالْخَبَائِثِ

Bismillāh, Allāhumma innī aʿūdhu bika min al-khubuthi wal-khabā'ith

In the name of Allah. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from evil and evildoers (male and female devils).

Upon exiting: غُفْرَانَكَ (Ghufrānak — I seek Your forgiveness)

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī & Muslim — narrated from Anas ibn Mālik (RA). The addition of Bismillāh is narrated by al-Tirmidhī from the ḥadīth of ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (RA).

Background

We have a du'ā for almost everything — eating, sleeping, travelling — and yes, even for the bathroom. The bathroom is one of the few places where dhikr is actually prohibited. So while you're in there, shayṭān is most active. We build a fortress before we enter. For the past 1,400 years, the bathroom was a single-function location — you got in and you got out. But now people chill in the bathroom, scroll, watch shows, and can spend up to an hour at a time there. It becomes even more important that you learn how to protect yourself.

Breaking Down the Du'ā

PointDetail
Why say Bismillāh before entering? The Prophet ﷺ said: "The barrier between the eyes of the jinn and the nakedness of the son of Adam when they enter to relieve themselves is to say Bismillāh." Anytime you remove your clothes, begin by saying Bismillāh — so you don't have an audience of jinn staring at you. This applies to the bathroom, the shower, and changing clothes.
What does الخُبُث وَالخَبَائِث mean? Two valid readings: (1) With ḍammah on both letters (al-khubuthu wal-khabā'ith) — meaning male and female devils. (2) With sukūn (al-khubth) — meaning evil itself. Ibn al-Anbārī said al-khubth with sukūn means disbelief, the worst kind of evil, and al-khabā'ith means the devils who drag you towards it. Either way, you're covering all bases: seeking refuge from evil and from those who promote evil.
Where exactly is dhikr prohibited? In modern bathrooms that combine the shower, toilet, and sink — only the area at the toilet itself takes the ruling. At the sink, you can mention Allah's name while making wuḍū'. When removing clothes for the shower, you can say Bismillāh. If you want to be on the safe side, say it before entering the bathroom entirely.
Why say غُفْرَانَكَ when leaving? You're acknowledging that Allah just relieved you of waste — if it stayed in your body, it would have killed you. But you also remember that your sins are toxins even more disastrous. So you're asking Allah to relieve you of your sins just as He relieved you of your bodily waste. That symbolism is incredible and profound.

Takeaway

This du'ā is mustaḥab (recommended) according to the consensus of the scholars. Imām an-Nawawī said it makes no difference whether you're entering a bathroom in a building or going out to the desert. The Prophet ﷺ didn't leave a single moment unprotected or unconnected — not eating, not sleeping, not travelling, and definitely not entering the bathroom. Build this habit this Ramadan: hand on the door, Bismillāh, Allāhumma innī aʿūdhu bika min al-khubuthi wal-khabā'ith. Walking out: Ghufrānak. These du'ās are training — building a lifestyle where every moment is infused with remembrance of Allah.

The Force Field of Morning & Evening

Watch

Dhikr

Bismillāh alladhī lā yaḍurru maʿa ismihi shay'un — the morning and evening protection dhikr

Theme

A dhikr that acts as a force field protecting you from all harm — say it three times after Fajr and three times after Maghrib

The Dhikr

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الَّذِي لَا يَضُرُّ مَعَ اسْمِهِ شَيْءٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

Bismillāhi alladhī lā yaḍurru maʿa ismihi shay'un fil-arḍi wa lā fis-samā', wa huwas-Samīʿ ul-ʿAlīm

In the name of Allah, with whose name nothing is harmed on earth nor in the heavens. And He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing.

Frequency: Three times after Fajr (protection until sunset) and three times after Maghrib (protection until Fajr).

Source: Sunan al-Tirmidhī, Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Sunan Ibn Mājah — narrated by ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān (RA). Graded authentic.

Background

Sh. Waleed bin Sunnī shared one of the most incredible stories about the power of this dhikr. During an ʿUmrah trip in Mecca, his friend Sāmī was possessed by a jinn. After hours of ruqyah, Sh. Waleed asked the voice: "My friend and I were holding his hands — why didn't you hit us?" The voice replied: "If I could have, I would have killed you. But between me and you both is a wall." He asked: "A wall?" The voice said: "You read Bismillāh alladhī lā yaḍurr…" — the jinn couldn't even finish saying the statement. Since age 14, Sh. Waleed had never seen a morning or evening without reciting this dhikr. He was being protected even as the jinn wanted to harm him.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
"There is no servant" — this is for everyone The Arabic uses the indefinite form preceded by مِن in the context of negation — indicating absolute generality. This dhikr applies to everyone: man, woman, young, old, righteous, struggling. Anyone who says it gets the protection.
Morning = after Fajr, Evening = after Maghrib Unlike most evening adhkār which scholars say begin after ʿAṣr, this specific dhikr says مَسَاءَ كُلِّ لَيْلَةٍ — "the evening of each night." The evening of any night isn't going to begin after ʿAṣr; it begins after sunset. Say it at the beginning of each period: after Fajr for the day, after Maghrib for the night.
"On earth nor in the heavens" — total coverage Everything that exists is either celestial or terrestrial. Nothing from above can harm you and nothing from below can harm you — whether jinn, humans, shayāṭīn, or any affliction from any direction. If Allah hears everything and knows everything, then He knows you, the one seeking refuge, and He knows what you're seeking refuge from.
The story of Abān ibn ʿUthmān Abān narrated this ḥadīth while himself suffering from partial paralysis. A listener looked confused — you're narrating about protection but you're paralyzed? Abān said: "This ḥadīth is as I have narrated to you, but I did not say it on that day, so Allah carried out His decree upon me." Imām al-Qurṭubī said something similar: "Since I heard this dhikr I practiced it, and nothing harmed me until a time I neglected it — I was stung by a scorpion in Madinah at night."

Takeaway

This dhikr is a shield — say it three times in the morning and three times in the evening, and the Prophet ﷺ guaranteed that nothing will harm you. It protects from sudden calamities: heart attacks, strokes, car accidents — moments where everything changes in an instant. Say it with certainty in your heart, be truly present when you say it, and never let a morning or evening pass without it. Don't be like Abān who missed one day and had a stroke. Don't be like al-Qurṭubī who forgot once and was stung by a scorpion. Build this fortress every single day.

Help Me Remember You

Watch

Dhikr

Allāhumma a'innī 'alā dhikrika wa shukrika wa ḥusni 'ibādatik — the du'ā the Prophet ﷺ taught Mu'ādh out of love

Theme

Asking Allah for help to remember Him, thank Him, and worship Him beautifully — the three pillars of servitude

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ أَعِنِّي عَلَى ذِكْرِكَ وَشُكْرِكَ وَحُسْنِ عِبَادَتِكَ

Allāhumma a'innī 'alā dhikrika wa shukrika wa ḥusni 'ibādatik

O Allah, help me to remember You, to thank You, and to worship You in the best manner.

When to say it: After every ṣalāh — scholars say before the taslīm (after tashahhud and ṣalāh upon the Prophet ﷺ), in the window where you make du'ā while still in prayer.

Source: Sunan Abī Dāwūd and Sunan al-Nasā'ī — narrated by Mu'ādh ibn Jabal (RA). The Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not fail to say this after every prayer."

Background

One day the Prophet ﷺ took Mu'ādh ibn Jabal's hand, drew his attention, and said: "O Mu'ādh, by Allah, I truly love you." He emphasised it — he swore by Allah, used "innī" and "la" for multiple layers of emphasis. Mu'ādh replied: "May my mother and father be sacrificed for you, Ya Rasūlullāh, and by Allah I love you too." Then the Prophet ﷺ gave him this instruction. What makes it even more beautiful is that Mu'ādh passed it on to his student prefacing it with "I love you," and his student did the same — each narrator transmitted not just the words, but the emotion. This hadith is called musalsal bil-muḥabba: a chain linked by love.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
"O Allah, help me" — starting with need You're not declaring you'll do something great on your own. You're admitting from the beginning: I need Your help. إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ — this is the essence of servitude. Recognising that every good deed you accomplish, every moment of remembrance, is only because Allah helped you.
ذِكْرِكَ — Help me remember You وَلَذِكْرُ اللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ — the dhikr of Allah is greater than everything. The Prophet ﷺ was asked: which of the Mujāhidīn has the greatest reward? He said: the one who remembers Allah the most. Which of those who fast? The same answer. For every category — charity, ṣalāh, ḥajj — the greatest reward goes to those who remember Allah the most.
شُكْرِكَ — Help me thank You Gratitude has three components: gratitude of the heart (love and turning to Allah), gratitude of the tongue (praising and thanking Him), and gratitude of the limbs (obedience and service). Allah says وَقَلِيلٌ مِّنْ عِبَادِيَ الشَّكُورُ — very few of My servants are grateful. He gave you everything you asked for — that house, that university, those children — even if you forgot your du'ā, He didn't forget.
حُسْنِ عِبَادَتِكَ — Help me worship You excellently This is the station of iḥsān: worshipping Allah as if you see Him, and if you can't, knowing He sees you. Not just to worship, but to worship with excellence, presence, quality, and khushū'. Allah says: لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا — He didn't say أَكْثَر (most deeds) but أَحْسَن (best deeds). We take pride in being top-rated in our careers — but am I top-rated in my worship?
The three lead to one another Dhikr leads to shukr: remembering Allah leads to gratitude. Shukr leads to iḥsān: لَئِنْ شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ — if you show gratitude, I will increase you. And that increase enables more beautiful worship. It's a virtuous cycle, and you need Allah's help to maintain it.

Takeaway

The Prophet ﷺ told Mu'ādh: "Do not fail to say this" — a prohibition, strong language out of love. Some scholars said it's obligatory; most say highly recommended. Either way, make it a habit for every ṣalāh this Ramadan. And pick one act of worship you already do — your morning adhkār, your Qur'ān, your night prayers — and ask: what would it look like to move it from a 6.5 to an 8? Not perfection, but just better. More focused. More present. More excellent. That's ḥusn al-'ibādah in action.

The Du'ā When Entering the Masjid

Watch

Dhikr

A'ūdhu billāhi al-'Aẓīm wa bi wajhihi al-karīm wa sulṭānihi al-qadīm min ash-Shayṭān ir-rajīm — protection from Shayṭān for the rest of your day

Theme

Seeking refuge in Allah's magnificence, noble face, and eternal authority when entering His most beloved place

The Dhikr

أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ الْعَظِيمِ وَبِوَجْهِهِ الْكَرِيمِ وَسُلْطَانِهِ الْقَدِيمِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ

A'ūdhu billāhi al-'Aẓīm, wa bi wajhihi al-karīm, wa sulṭānihi al-qadīm, min ash-Shayṭān ir-rajīm

I seek refuge in Allah the Great, in His noble face, and in His eternal authority, from Shayṭān the accursed.

When to say it: At the threshold of the masjid, just before stepping inside.

Source: Sunan Abī Dāwūd — narrated by 'Abdullāh ibn 'Amr ibn al-'Āṣ (RA). The Prophet ﷺ said that when you say this, Shayṭān says: "He has been protected from me for the rest of the day."

Background

The masjid is the place Allah loves most, yet the du'ā to enter it is a grand fortress — far more intense than the light dhikr said when entering the marketplace (the place Allah hates most). Why? Because Shayṭān isn't worried about the people in the market — they'll take each other to Jahannam on autopilot. His focus is on the believers who pray. He has given up hope of being worshipped by them, but what remains is instigation between them — the politics, the backbiting, the egos. This du'ā protects you from all of that.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ الْعَظِيمِ — seeking refuge in the Great A'ūdhu means "I seek protection, I take shelter." You're not exposed and vulnerable — you place yourself under Allah's care. العَظِيم is a comprehensive attribute: magnificence requires sovereignty, authority, absolute richness, strength, and power all combined. His greatness towers over everything, including Shayṭān.
وَبِوَجْهِهِ الْكَرِيمِ — and in His noble face A real attribute that befits His Majesty — not metaphorical. The proof it's distinct from His essence: you already sought refuge in Allah at the start, so mentioning His face adds specificity, not repetition. You are being more emphatic — seeking refuge in Allah's noble, perfect face.
وَسُلْطَانِهِ الْقَدِيمِ — and in His eternal authority His sulṭān: authority, management, power, subjugation — everything is under His control. القَدِيم means eternal — Allah is the First, nothing before Him. Note: Al-Qadīm is not a name of Allah (not found in Qur'ān or authentic Sunnah as a name), but it appears here as an attribute of His authority.
مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ — from the outcast Shayṭān الرَّجِيم means the outcasted, the stoned — expelled from Allah's mercy, stoned with His curse, with shooting stars, and with the curses of creation. In Sūrat al-Falaq you seek one attribute of Allah against four evils; in Sūrat an-Nās you seek three attributes against Shayṭān alone — showing how formidable an enemy he is.
Why so grand for the masjid? There's a specific shayṭān called Khinzab, designated for distracting you in prayer (Muslim). Shayṭān has given up hope of being worshipped by those who pray, so he works on instigation: masjid politics, judging, backbiting. Meanwhile, other venues may feel "welcoming" — but Shayṭān already won them on the big stuff.

Takeaway

Every time you enter the masjid — whether five times a day or once in a while — this du'ā should be on your tongue. You're not just walking into a building; you're entering the place Shayṭān fights hardest to corrupt. Three attributes of Allah form your fortress: His magnificence, His noble face, and His eternal authority. Say it at the threshold and you're guaranteed protection for the rest of your day.

Better Than a Servant

Watch

Dhikr

SubḥānAllāh ×33, Alḥamdulillāh ×33, Allāhu Akbar ×34 — the dhikr the Prophet ﷺ gave Fāṭimah and 'Alī before sleep

Theme

Dhikr as fuel for the body — remembrance of Allah gives physical strength and energy beyond any worldly help

The Dhikr

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ × ٣٣ · الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ × ٣٣ · اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ × ٣٤

SubḥānAllāh ×33 · Alḥamdulillāh ×33 · Allāhu Akbar ×34

Glory be to Allah (×33) · All praise is due to Allah (×33) · Allah is the Greatest (×34)

When to say it: Before going to sleep, when you take to your bed.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim — narrated by 'Alī ibn Abī Ṭālib (RA). The Prophet ﷺ told Fāṭimah and 'Alī: "This is better for you than a servant."

Background

Fāṭimah (RA), the leader of the women of Paradise, came to her father's house with calloused hands from grinding grain, marks on her neck from carrying water skins, and a face changed from the smoke of baking. Her husband 'Alī (RA) was so exhausted from drawing water that he had chest pains. When she asked the Prophet ﷺ for a servant, he came to their home that night, sat between them, and instead gave them something better: this dhikr. He didn't dismiss their struggle or tell them to simply be patient — he gave them a real, practical solution rooted in remembrance of Allah.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
"Better" in what way? Some scholars say it's better for the ākhirah — the reward far outweighs any worldly benefit. Others say it's better in a worldly sense too: when you say this dhikr consistently, Allah gives you strength to carry burdens and complete tasks that would normally exhaust you. They extracted from this: ذِكْرُ اللَّهِ يُقَوِّي الْبَدَنَ — the remembrance of Allah strengthens the body.
Ibn al-Qayyim witnessed it in Ibn Taymiyyah Ibn al-Qayyim says: "Dhikr gives the one who remembers strength to the extent that he does what he could not imagine doing without it." He witnessed Ibn Taymiyyah sit after Fajr remembering Allah until almost midday. When Ibn Taymiyyah realized his student was waiting, he said: "This is my breakfast — without it, I'm not able to do much." He would write in one day what a scribe would write in a week.
'Alī never missed a night Decades later, 'Alī (RA) said: "Ever since I heard this from the Prophet ﷺ, I never went to sleep without saying it." Someone asked: "Not even on the day of Ṣiffīn?" — the bloodiest day in Muslim history, when more Muslims died than in all previous campaigns combined. 'Alī said: "Not even on the day of Ṣiffīn." The Ṣaḥābah didn't just learn — they acted, and they acted consistently every day of their lives.
Don't impose what others can't bear The Prophet ﷺ didn't tell Fāṭimah to just be patient — he came to their home and gave them a practical solution with compassion. Lesson: hold your family to obligations, but with voluntary acts, encourage without imposing. You don't want your family to resent worship because it's forced beyond their capacity.

Takeaway

Don't go to sleep without saying this dhikr. Whether you're working a physically demanding job, nursing, teaching, raising children, or fasting in Ramadan — dhikr is fuel. People who say it every night wake up with stamina others don't have. The Ṣaḥābah's distance from us is that when they learned something, they never stopped doing it. Make this your non-negotiable before bed, even on your hardest day.

The Du'ā of Istikhāra

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā al-Istikhāra — the tool the Prophet ﷺ taught his companions for every decision, taught like a sūrah of the Qur'ān

Theme

Seeking Allah's guidance and blessing in every decision — submitting to His knowledge, power, and wisdom over your own

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْتَخِيرُكَ بِعِلْمِكَ وَأَسْتَقْدِرُكَ بِقُدْرَتِكَ وَأَسْأَلُكَ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ الْعَظِيمِ فَإِنَّكَ تَقْدِرُ وَلَا أَقْدِرُ وَتَعْلَمُ وَلَا أَعْلَمُ وَأَنْتَ عَلَّامُ الْغُيُوبِ · اللَّهُمَّ إِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنَّ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ خَيْرٌ لِي فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي فَاقْدُرْهُ لِي وَيَسِّرْهُ لِي ثُمَّ بَارِكْ لِي فِيهِ · وَإِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنَّ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ شَرٌّ لِي فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي فَاصْرِفْهُ عَنِّي وَاصْرِفْنِي عَنْهُ وَاقْدُرْ لِيَ الْخَيْرَ حَيْثُ كَانَ ثُمَّ أَرْضِنِي بِهِ

Allāhumma innī astakhīruka bi 'ilmika wa astaqdiruka bi qudratika wa as'aluka min faḍlika al-'aẓīm, fa innaka taqdiru wa lā aqdir, wa ta'lamu wa lā a'lam, wa anta 'allām ul-ghuyūb. Allāhumma in kunta ta'lam anna hādha al-amr khayrun lī fī dīnī wa ma'āshī wa 'āqibati amrī, faqdurhu lī wa yassirhu lī thumma bārik lī fīh. Wa in kunta ta'lam anna hādha al-amr sharrun lī fī dīnī wa ma'āshī wa 'āqibati amrī, faṣrifhu 'annī waṣrifnī 'anhu, waqdur lī al-khayra ḥaythu kān, thumma arḍinī bih.

O Allah, I seek Your guidance through Your knowledge and I seek ability through Your power and I ask You for Your immense bounty. For You have power and I am powerless, You have knowledge and I don't, and You are the Knower of the unseen. O Allah, if You know this matter is good for me in my religion, livelihood, and outcome, then decree it for me, facilitate it, and bless me in it. And if You know it is bad for me in my religion, livelihood, and outcome, then turn it away from me and turn me away from it, and decree for me good wherever it may be, then make me pleased with it.

When to say it: After praying two voluntary rak'ahs — sit and make this du'ā after completing the prayer.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī — narrated by Jābir ibn 'Abdillāh (RA). "The Prophet ﷺ used to teach us istikhāra in all matters just like he would teach us a sūrah from the Qur'ān."

Background

You're standing at a crossroads — two job offers, a marriage decision, a life-altering choice. You've made your pros and cons list, talked to everyone, and you're still paralysed. Istikhāra is not about Allah showing you a sign or giving you a dream. It's about making your best decision and then asking Allah to bless it. You still make the choice — but this du'ā ensures that whatever you decide is guided by His knowledge and power, not limited by your own.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
أَسْتَخِيرُكَ بِعِلْمِكَ — seeking khayr through His knowledge Istikhāra literally means seeking what is khayr (good). You're not saying "give me this thing" — you're saying "I don't know where the good is; You do; guide me to it." Deep humility: even if you're an expert in the field, you still don't know what Allah knows.
تَقْدِرُ وَلَا أَقْدِرُ — You have power, I don't You negate two things from yourself: power and knowledge. عَلَّامُ الْغُيُوبِ is the intensive form — He knows the past, present, future, and what would have been if things were different. He knows every combination and permutation of your life.
فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي — the three checks Religion comes first, then livelihood, then the long-term outcome (dunyā and ākhirah). You're asking Allah to look at the full picture — the one you can't see. If it checks all boxes: decree it, facilitate it, put barakah in it.
فَاصْرِفْهُ عَنِّي وَاصْرِفْنِي عَنْهُ — turn it and turn me The door might close (turn it away from me), but you might still be staring at that closed door. So you also ask: turn me away from it — let my heart lose interest. "You can't fire me because I quit." And then: ثُمَّ أَرْضِنِي بِهِ — the word ثُمَّ indicates delay. It might take time, but eventually make me look back and say alḥamdulillāh.
Common mistakes ① Treating istikhāra as waiting for a sign or dream — there's no basis for that. You pray, gather information, consult, decide, and move forward. If the door closes, that's the result. ② Being devastated if it doesn't work out — you asked Allah to turn away what's bad and make you pleased with His decree. Being bitter means you missed the entire point.

Takeaway

Istikhāra isn't just for the big decisions. The Prophet ﷺ taught it for everything — you don't know what trivial decision contains your greatest blessings or your greatest trials. Imagine constantly asking Allah for guidance: how many wrong turns would you have avoided? If you don't have the du'ā memorised in Arabic yet, you can say it in English — but work towards memorising it with the precision of a sūrah, because that's how Jābir said the Prophet ﷺ taught it.

The Eight Enemies of Success

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā seeking refuge from eight destructive traits — distress, sorrow, incapacity, laziness, cowardice, miserliness, crushing debt, and being overpowered

Theme

Identifying and fighting the internal and external enemies that hold you back from becoming who you're meant to be

The Dhikr

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ وَالْجُبْنِ وَالْبُخْلِ وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ

Allāhumma innī a'ūdhu bika min al-hammi wal-ḥazan, wal-'ajzi wal-kasal, wal-jubni wal-bukhl, wa ḍala'id-dayn wa ghalabatir-rijāl.

O Allah, I seek refuge in You from distress and sorrow, from incapacity and laziness, from cowardice and miserliness, and from the burden of debt and being overpowered by men.

When to say it: Daily — the Prophet ﷺ would say this du'ā regularly as part of his routine.

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī — narrated by Anas ibn Mālik (RA).

Background

What's actually stopping you from becoming who you're supposed to be? If you examine the gap between who you are and who you could be, you'll find it's usually not external circumstances — it's internal. Your own traits, habits, and spiritual diseases are holding you back. The Prophet ﷺ taught us a du'ā seeking refuge from eight specific things — eight enemies of success that ruin a person's dunyā and ākhirah. The scholars point out these eight things come in four pairs, each connected.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
Pair 1: الهَمّ وَالحَزَن — Distress & Sorrow Both are pain of the soul, but from different directions. Al-Hamm (distress/anxiety) is about the future — worrying about what might happen. Al-Ḥazan (sorrow) is about the past — grief over what already happened. People spend their entire lives bouncing between these two, never present. The rational person discards what is past and leaves the future to Allah. As the poet said: "If I awake with the essentials for my day, don't invoke the concerns of tomorrow — tomorrow will have new sustenance."
Why seek refuge from sorrow? Ibn al-Qayyim says sorrow weakens the heart, drains resolve, and damages willpower. Nothing is more beloved to Shayṭān than a believer's sorrow. When you're depressed or anxious, you can't worship, work, or build anything meaningful. Allah negated sorrow from the people of Jannah: "Praise to Allah who has removed from us all sorrow" (Fāṭir 35:34).
Pair 2: العَجْز وَالكَسَل — Incapacity & Laziness Al-'Ajz is when you literally can't do something — you lack the physical ability, strength, or resources. Al-Kasal is when you could do something but don't want to. The hypocrites are described: "When they stand for prayer, they stand lazily" (An-Nisā' 4:142). Many of us are lazy about Qur'ān, ṣalāh, work, and responsibilities. We have ability but lack motivation, discipline, and drive.
Pair 3: الجُبْن وَالبُخْل — Cowardice & Miserliness Both are forms of withholding. Cowardice is withholding yourself — too scared to do what's right. Miserliness is withholding wealth — gripping onto it so tight that nothing flows through. Both come from the same place: fear and scarcity, clinging to what you have instead of trusting Allah. The courageous and generous are free and content; the coward and miser are paranoid, anxious, and never enjoy what they're protecting.
Khālid ibn al-Walīd's lesson When Khālid (RA) was dying, he said: "There's not a part of my body except that it has a scar from a spear, lance, arrow, or sword — yet I'm dying in my bed." Putting yourself in danger doesn't necessarily cause your death. Allah's qadar determines how a person dies. Khālid sought violent death 100 times over, yet was written to die peacefully.
Pair 4: ضَلَع الدَّيْن وَغَلَبَة الرِّجَال — Debt & Being Overpowered These are external pressures that crush you. Debt so heavy you can't stand up straight — you're hiding from creditors, your life consumed by avoidance. Being overpowered — when people dominate, oppress, or subjugate you. Both strip you of dignity and freedom.
The big picture: these eight cover everything Distress and sorrow = pain of the soul. Incapacity and laziness = failure of the body and will. Cowardice and miserliness = corruption of character. Debt and being overpowered = external oppression. That's soul, body, character, and circumstances — a person's entire existence. And six of the eight are internal. The real battle isn't out there — it's in your soul, habits, and thought patterns.

Takeaway

Say this du'ā regularly — daily. Mean it. Think about what you're seeking refuge from, then go and attack the things you find within yourself: laziness, cowardice, stinginess, anxiety. You can't fix these things on your own. We all need Allah's protection from our own weaknesses. Make this a daily part of your routine and watch how it changes the way you see your own obstacles.

The Du'ā of Travel

Watch

Dhikr

Du'ā of travel — a comprehensive supplication that transforms every journey into worship

Theme

Turning travel from something mundane or stressful into a spiritual act that connects you to Allah at every stage

The Dhikr

اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ ، اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ · سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُ مُقْرِنِينَ · وَإِنَّا إِلَى رَبِّنَا لَمُنْقَلِبُونَ · اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّا نَسْأَلُكَ فِي سَفَرِنَا هَذَا الْبِرَّ وَالتَّقْوَى وَمِنَ الْعَمَلِ مَا تَرْضَى · اللَّهُمَّ هَوِّنْ عَلَيْنَا سَفَرَنَا هَذَا وَاطْوِ عَنَّا بُعْدَهُ · اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ الصَّاحِبُ فِي السَّفَرِ وَالْخَلِيفَةُ فِي الْأَهْلِ · اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ وَعْثَاءِ السَّفَرِ وَكَآبَةِ الْمَنْظَرِ وَسُوءِ الْمُنْقَلَبِ فِي الْمَالِ وَالْأَهْلِ

Allāhu Akbar, Allāhu Akbar, Allāhu Akbar. Subḥāna-lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa mā kunnā lahū muqrinīn, wa innā ilā Rabbinā la-munqalibūn. Allāhumma innā nas'aluka fī safarinā hādha al-birra wat-taqwā wa min al-'amali mā tarḍā. Allāhumma hawwin 'alaynā safaranā hādhā waṭwi 'annā bu'dah. Allāhumma anta-ṣ-ṣāḥibu fis-safar wal-khalīfatu fil-ahl. Allāhumma innī a'ūdhu bika min wa'thā'is-safar wa ka'ābatil-manẓar wa sū'il-munqalabi fil-māli wal-ahl.

Allah is the Greatest (×3). Exalted is the One who subjected this for us, and we could never have subdued it, and indeed to our Lord we will return. O Allah, we ask You on this journey for righteousness and piety, and for deeds pleasing to You. O Allah, make easy for us this journey and shorten its distance. O Allah, You are the Companion on the journey and the Successor over the family. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the hardships of travel, from a distressing sight, and from finding family and property in misfortune upon return.

When to say it: Upon mounting your ride (car, plane, etc.) at the start of a journey. Upon returning, add: آيِبُونَ تَائِبُونَ عَابِدُونَ لِرَبِّنَا حَامِدُونَ — "Returning, repenting, worshipping, and praising our Lord."

Source: Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim — narrated by 'Abdullāh ibn 'Umar (RA).

Background

A young man was traveling to a city to engage in something ḥarām. He recited the du'ā of travel and when he reflected on the words, he decided to turn around. Most people think of travel as an adventure or a hassle — but travel is spiritual. The Prophet ﷺ described it as "a piece of torment," but it's also an opportunity to remember Allah in a specific way, to seek His protection, and to turn every journey into an act of worship.

Breaking Down the Dhikr

PointDetail
اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ — three times upon mounting The Prophet ﷺ would say Allāhu Akbar three times when mounting his riding animal. Why? (1) Because he is ascending/elevated — and his pattern was to say takbīr when ascending. (2) Remembering the greatness of Allah who subjected this animal to him. (3) Because it pleased him, and he would say takbīr when something pleased him.
سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَذَا — "this" is timeless Notice the word "this" (هَذَا). He doesn't say the camel, car, or plane — just "this." That makes the du'ā timeless. You get in a car — thousands of pounds of metal moving at 70 mph and you just steer it. You board a plane — hundreds of tons of steel at 30,000 feet and you're watching a movie. That's subjugation from Allah. Without His subjugation, you'd be helpless.
وَإِنَّا إِلَى رَبِّنَا لَمُنْقَلِبُونَ — the reality check Think about how much you prepared for this small journey — comparing flights, booking hotels, planning itineraries. "You only live once," so you take advantage. But what about the real journey you're on — the one to your Lord? How much investment do you put into that day-to-day?
The economy-class analogy You fly economy while others fly business or first class. Do you sit burning with envy? Most of us don't — it's only a few hours before we all arrive at the same destination. This dunyā is like that. Some travel through it first class, some economy. But it's a short trip and then we all reach our destination. Allah says: "The Hereafter is greater in degrees of difference and greater in distinction" (Al-Isrā' 17:21).
البِرَّ وَالتَّقْوَى — righteousness and piety on the road Travel is when people get lax. You're in a new place, nobody knows you, you're away from your community. "What happens in Vegas stays in Vegas" — that's the mentality of anonymity. But this du'ā commits you to the opposite: obedience even when nobody's watching. Especially when nobody's watching.
أَنْتَ الصَّاحِبُ فِي السَّفَرِ وَالْخَلِيفَةُ فِي الْأَهْلِ You're calling Allah your companion on the road and the one protecting your family while you're gone. الصَّاحِب — the only one truly with you on your entire life's journey. الخَلِيفَة — from khalafa, "what's behind you." You're saying: "I've made the arrangements, but ultimately You're the one protecting them."
وَعْثَاء السَّفَرِ وَكَآبَةِ المَنْظَرِ وَسُوءِ المُنْقَلَبِ Three things you seek refuge from: (1) Wa'thā' — the hardship and exhaustion of travel, even smooth trips tire you out. (2) Ka'ābat al-manẓar — a distressing sight, whether it's your travel companion's true colours or something painful at your destination. (3) Sū' al-munqalab — returning to find your family changed, your business collapsed, or disaster at home. You ask Allah to protect everything you care about while you're gone.

Takeaway

This du'ā should stop you in your tracks if you're traveling to disobey Allah. You're saying "O Allah, I ask You for righteousness and piety" — if you're headed somewhere ḥarām, these words should make you rethink the entire trip. Learn this du'ā, appreciate its meanings, and say it every time you travel. It transforms the mundane into worship and the stressful into connection with Allah.
